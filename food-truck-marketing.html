Food truck marketing: the honest playbook.

A food truck's marketing problem is unique: your restaurant moves. Every strategy flows from that one fact — if people can't find you today, nothing else matters. Here's the playbook.

Broadcast the location like it's the product

Because it is. Today's spot and hours everywhere, every day: social bio, morning story post, Google Business Profile updates, and a simple site with the weekly schedule as real text. The trucks that win treat the schedule like a menu item; the ones that struggle make fans guess. Consistency beats reach — a smaller audience that always knows where you are outbuys a big one that doesn't.

Appetite content, shot at the window

Food video is the strongest content in the game and you're cooking it fresh daily: the steam, the flip, the pull-apart, the line. Fifteen-second phone clips at service, posted at decision hours (11am–1pm, 5–7pm), with the location in the caption. One good line-out-the-window clip does more than a month of graphics.

The bookings side most trucks under-market

Catering and private events are the margin business — office lunches, weddings, breweries. Give that a page of its own with real photos, what's included, and an inquiry path. Event organizers search like customers do: if “food truck catering [your area]” finds a readable page with proof, you get inquiries no amount of street parking produces.

Questions people actually ask

How do food trucks announce their location?

Everywhere, redundantly: morning social post, updated bio, Google profile update, and a weekly schedule page on a simple site. Machines read the site and profile; fans read the stories. The trucks that skip the machine-readable half stay invisible to searches like “food trucks near me today.”

What social media works best for food trucks?

Instagram and TikTok for the appetite clips, posted at decision hours. But the real answer is wherever your actual customers check — ask the window line. One platform posted daily with real food beats three updated weekly with graphics.

How do food trucks get catering gigs?

A dedicated catering page with photos, packages, and a fast inquiry path — then reviews from every event asked at the happy moment. Organizers compare options online like anyone else; the trucks with readable proof win the search, and one wedding pays for a slow street week.

Do food trucks need a website?

A simple one, absolutely: schedule, menu as text, catering page, contact. It's what Google and AI assistants read to recommend you — social alone is invisible to them, and platform reach can vanish overnight. Five fast pages cover a truck completely.
