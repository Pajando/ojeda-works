Paying for listing videos that don't win you the next listing? Real estate video: what works, honestly.

You pay for a slick listing tour, the home sells, and the next seller still calls someone else. Two different things get called “real estate video”: the listing tour that sells a home, and the agent video that sells you. My take: if you only ever make the first kind, you're skipping the one that sticks around after the sale.

Listing videos: the property sellers

Prices for walkthrough tours vary by market, by shooter, and by extras like drone or twilight shots, so get quotes in writing. My take: a full tour matters most for homes with something special to show. For a standard listing, strong photos plus a short vertical clip for social may be enough.

Agent-brand videos: the asset that doesn't expire

A listing video is done when the listing is. An agent video, about who you are, what you're like to work with and what you know about your market, can keep working on your site and your profiles long after any one sale. No single deal forces you to make it, which is exactly why it's easy to skip.

Start with short videos answering what buyers and sellers in your area actually ask. They show you know the market instead of just saying it. Put the answers in writing on your site too. Google says its AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special tricks, and its basic practices include making important content available as text and supporting that text with good images and videos (Google Search Central).

Where this connects to being found

A video alone won't get you found. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search, and that there's no way to pay for a better local ranking (Google Business Profile Help). So keep your profiles complete and your info the same everywhere, and let the video build trust once people find you. Questions people actually ask

How much does a real estate listing video cost?

It depends on your market, the shooter, the size of the home, and extras like drone or twilight shots. I won't quote a market average I can't back up. Get two or three written quotes and compare what's included: length, formats, edits and who owns the footage.

Do listing videos actually sell homes faster?

I don't have solid proof that video alone sells a home faster, so I won't claim it. Video can show how the rooms connect in a way photos can't. My take: price and the home itself do most of the work, and video helps a well-priced listing more than it rescues an overpriced one.

What video should a new real estate agent make first?

I'd start with a video about you, not a listing. A new agent may not have listings yet, but you can always tell your own story and share what you know about your market. Short answers to real local questions keep working after any one sale closes.

Vertical or horizontal for real estate video?

Both, from one shoot if you can: vertical clips for phones and social, horizontal for your website and bigger screens. Ask for both before the shoot. Shooting only one limits where you can use the footage.
