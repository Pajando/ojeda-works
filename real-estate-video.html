Real estate video: what works, what it costs.

Two different videos get called “real estate video”: the listing tour that sells a property, and the agent video that sells you. Most agents buy the first and skip the second — backwards, since the listing expires and you don't.

Listing videos: the property sellers

Walkthrough tours commonly run a few hundred dollars per property from local shooters, more with drone coverage and twilight shots. They matter most for distinctive properties; for standard listings, strong photos plus a short vertical clip for social commonly do the same job for less.

Agent-brand videos: the asset that doesn't expire

A listing video dies with the listing. An agent video — who you are, what you're like to work with, what you know about your market — works for years, on your site, your profiles, and every listing page. It's the piece most agents skip because no single transaction demands it, and it's the piece that compounds.

The market-knowledge angle works hardest: short videos answering what buyers and sellers in your area actually ask. They prove expertise instead of claiming it, and they're exactly the content search engines and AI assistants can surface when someone asks about your market.

Where this connects to being found

Video supports visibility; it doesn't create it alone. The agent whose site and profiles are structured so Google and AI models can read them gets recommended — the video then closes the trust gap. Questions people actually ask

How much does a real estate listing video cost?

Commonly a few hundred dollars per property for a walkthrough from a local shooter, with drone footage and twilight shots adding more. High-end cinematic tours for luxury listings run into the thousands — justified by the listing price, not the norm.

Do listing videos actually sell homes faster?

Video gives buyers a spatial sense photos can't, and listings with video attract more attention online. But the honest driver of sale speed is price and property; video amplifies interest in a well-priced listing rather than rescuing an overpriced one.

What video should a new real estate agent make first?

The agent-brand video, not a listing video — new agents rarely control listings, but they fully control their own story and market knowledge. Short answers to real local questions build the asset that keeps working after any individual listing closes.

Vertical or horizontal for real estate video?

Both, from one shoot: vertical clips for social feeds where buyers scroll, horizontal for the listing page and site. Shooting only one format is the most common avoidable mistake — it halves where the footage can live.
