Video

Your event happens once. Event videographer cost, and how not to lose the footage.

Your launch, party or big night happens once. If the video fails, there's no redo. My take: quotes can run from a few hundred dollars for a short, simple event to a few thousand for a full day with more than one camera. The cheapest quote can carry a risk the price doesn't show. Here's how to read the price and protect your footage.

How event videography is priced

Hourly quotes cover time on site. Ask if editing is included or billed on top. Packages bundle filming and an edited video, like a short highlight film. My take: compare the total price for the finished, delivered video, not the hourly rate. More rooms or more stages means more cameras, and the budget follows. Rates vary a lot by town and experience, so get two or three local quotes before you decide.

What drives the price

Hours on site, number of shooters, what you get back (a highlight reel? the full speeches? a same-day edit?), sound, and how fast you get it. My take: speeches and toasts need a real microphone on the speaker or the sound board, not just the mic on the camera. The hidden cost is backup: a second camera, extra batteries, recording to two cards at once. Events don't repeat, so ask what backup is in the quote.

The questions that protect you

Ask to see two full event videos, not just highlight reels. Ask who exactly shows up. It may not be the person whose work you saw. Ask how sound gets recorded for the speeches. Get the delivery date in writing, and ask what happens to the raw footage. And ask about backups. My take: that answer tells you more than the demo reel, because your event has no second take.

Quick answers

How much does a videographer cost for a 4-hour event?

It depends on your town, the shooter's experience and what you want back. My take: don't compare hourly rates, because editing may be extra. Get one total price for the finished, delivered video, and confirm what it includes: how long the highlight video is, whether full speeches are included, and how many rounds of changes you get.

Is one videographer enough for an event?

My take: for a small event in one room, one is usually enough. If two things happen at once, like a stage and the crowd's reaction, or two rooms, one person can't film both. That's what you pay a second shooter for: the moment you'd otherwise lose.

Why does event video cost more than regular video?

No retakes. My take: a good event shooter brings backup gear, like a second camera, extra batteries and two memory cards, and has to get once-only moments with clean sound. A promo shoot can be redone tomorrow. Your event can't, and the price reflects who carries that risk.

Can I just have a friend film my event?

For casual memories, sure. For anything you'd hate to lose, like speeches, a launch or a big birthday, the risk changes. One bad memory card or dead battery and it's gone. If money is tight, hire a pro for the most important hour and let phones cover the rest.
