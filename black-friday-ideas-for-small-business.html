Can't beat the big stores on price? Black Friday ideas that fit a small business.

Some of your regulars will spend their Black Friday money at the big chains. You can't beat them on price, and you don't have to. My take: offer what a big store can't copy, like your time, your bundles, your people. Here are the ideas, the timeline, and what to skip.

Offers big retail can't copy

Gift cards with a bonus (buy $50, get $60 of value): cash now, visits later, and no cut to your posted price. Bundles of your services or products at a package price feel generous without racing to the bottom. VIP early access for your email list and regulars turns the day into loyalty fuel. Book-now-for-January offers can help fill a slow January with November's buying energy.

My take: add value to the purchase instead of cutting your core price. A 40%-off sign can teach customers that your normal price isn't real.

The honest timeline

Early November: decide the one offer and get it on your site and Google Business Profile, written as real text Google can read, not just a picture. Two weeks out: email your list first (my take: they're the people most likely to show up), then social. Week of: post daily, with real photos and the real end date of the offer. Why start early? Google says crawling a new page can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. My take: if you wait until Wednesday night, all you have left is price, and that's a fight with the giant online stores you won't win.

What to skip

My take: skip doorbusters that lose money on the hope of upsells. Skip discounting your main service; bundle it instead. And skip ads for a generic “Black Friday sale!” If you run ads at all, run them on one specific offer, with a small budget, aimed at people who already know you.

Questions people actually ask

Should a service business do Black Friday at all?

Yes, just differently. Gift cards, bundled packages, and book-early-for-next-year offers turn November's buying mood into future visits without cutting your hourly rate. A gift-a-service angle can also reach holiday shoppers who would not normally book you.

How much should a small business discount on Black Friday?

My take: less than your gut says. Deep discounts can teach customers to wait for the sale, and they eat the margin the season is supposed to build. Try added value first, like a bonus, a bundle, or early access, so your normal price stays believable.

When should I start promoting a Black Friday offer?

Get the offer live on your site and Google profile by early November, tell your email list about two weeks before, and go loud on social the week of. Google says crawling a page can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks, so a page posted Thursday night may not show up in search in time.

Do Black Friday pages help my website the rest of the year?

My take: if you build one, keep the same web address every year and update the page each season, instead of deleting it and starting a new one every November.
