Black Friday ideas that actually fit a small business.

You can't out-discount the big boxes — and you don't need to. Small businesses win Black Friday with offers big retail can't copy. Here are the ideas that work, the timeline, and what to skip.

Offers big retail can't copy

Gift cards with a bonus (buy $50, get $60 of value): cash now, visits later, margin intact. Bundles of your services or products at a package price feel generous without racing to the bottom. VIP early access for your email list and regulars turns the day into loyalty fuel. Book-now-for-January offers fill your slowest month with November's buying energy.

The rule under all of them: discount the *experience of buying*, not your core price. A 40%-off sign teaches customers your normal price is fake.

The honest timeline

Early November: decide the one offer and get it on your site and Google Business Profile — as real text machines can read, not just a graphic. Two weeks out: email your list first (they're the ones who'll actually come), then social. Week of: post daily, real photos, countdowns. The businesses that win planned in October; the ones that improvise on Wednesday night compete with Amazon on price, which is the one fight to never pick.

What to skip

Doorbusters that lose money hoping for upsells that never come. Discounting your flagship service — service businesses do better bundling than discounting. And buying ads for a generic “Black Friday sale!” — if you do run ads, run them on the one specific offer, at a small budget, to the audience that already knows you.

Questions people actually ask

Should a service business do Black Friday at all?

Yes — differently. Gift cards, bundled packages, and book-early-for-next-year offers convert November's buying mood into future revenue without discounting your hourly value. A “gift a service” angle also captures holiday shoppers a service business normally never sees.

How much should a small business discount on Black Friday?

Less than instinct says. Deep discounts train customers to wait for them and gut the margin the season is supposed to build. Added value — bonuses, bundles, early access — commonly outperforms deep percentage cuts for small businesses, and protects your pricing integrity.

When should I start promoting a Black Friday offer?

Get the offer live on your site and Google profile by early November, tell your email list about two weeks before, and go loud on social the week of. Search engines and AI assistants need the lead time to read the offer — a page posted Thursday night is invisible to them.

Do Black Friday pages help my website the rest of the year?

If you build one, keep the URL alive year to year and update it each season — recurring seasonal pages build search history instead of starting from zero every November. A deleted-and-recreated page throws that compounding away.
