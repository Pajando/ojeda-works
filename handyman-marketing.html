Handyman marketing: the honest playbook.

A handyman's competition isn't other handymen — it's the homeowner's despair that anyone will ever call back. Show up when you said, for the price you said, and you're already in the top ten percent. Marketing just makes that reliability visible before the first job.

Make reliability visible

The homeowner's fear is specific: no-shows, vague pricing, disappearing mid-project. Answer it in public — reviews that say “replied in an hour, came Thursday like he said,” photos of finished punch lists, a real name and face. A handyman with 75 reviews averaging “showed up, fixed it, charged what he quoted” never has an empty week. Ask for the review while the fixed thing is still the best part of their day.

The list is the business

Every house that let you in once has ten more things breaking. Repeat clients and their referrals are the entire growth engine: keep a simple list, check in seasonally (“gutter season — want on the schedule?”), and treat small jobs as auditions for the big ones. Property managers and realtors are the multiplier — one relationship equals a monthly stream of work orders, won by being the one who documents and doesn't need chasing.

The foundation and what to skip

Google Business Profile with photos of real work, services listed plainly (what you do and what you don't), your towns, and whether you're licensed for the bigger stuff — plus a one-page site with the same in machine-readable text, because “reliable handyman near me” is exactly what people ask AI assistants. What to skip: competing on hourly rate against the guy who won't show up anyway, and shared-lead apps as the whole plan — the fees stack, and you're bidding for strangers while your past clients forget your number. Stay in front of the list instead.

Questions people actually ask

How does a handyman get more clients?

Reviews proving reliability, a complete profile, and systematic repeat business — seasonal check-ins with past clients and one or two property manager relationships fill most calendars without paid leads.

Should a handyman charge hourly or by the job?

By the job where scope is clear — homeowners hate open meters, and flat quotes win against vague hourly bids. Keep hourly for genuinely unpredictable work, and either way put the number in writing before starting.

Do handyman apps like TaskRabbit or Thumbtack work?

As a starter or gap-filler, yes; as the business, no. Fees compound and the platform owns the client. Use them to seed the review base and the repeat-client list, then let the list take over.

Does a handyman need a website?

One page, yes: services, towns, photos, reviews, license info, and how to reach you — in text search engines and AI assistants can read. It's the difference between being findable and being a rumor.
