Empty chairs on a Tuesday? Salon marketing ideas that actually fill them.

Salons have two things many businesses wish they had: work people can see, and clients who come back. My take: a lot of salon marketing wastes both. Here's what fills chairs, in order.

First: the free foundation

Before any paid idea: a complete Google Business Profile with your real work in the photos, services with prices listed, current hours, and a steady stream of honest reviews. Ask every client at the mirror moment, right after they see the result. This is what Google and AI apps can read when someone searches “best salon near me.” My take: many profiles are only half done, so being the complete one is a free edge.

Social that books instead of decorates

My take: the posts that book are your actual work: before-and-afters, transformations in motion, the chair-side process. Post the work, name the service and city in the caption, and make booking one tap from the profile. Client permission first, always. Two to four real posts a week beat daily filler, and transformation videos are the ones to make first.

Repeat and referral: the underused engine

Ask about rebooking before the client leaves, offer a simple 'bring a friend' perk, and send a monthly email with one real offer. My take: keeping clients costs little, and a salon lives on the second visit, not the first. Paid ads come last, and only pointed at a specific offer (a new-client special, a quiet-day promotion) once the foundation is set.

Questions people actually ask

How do I get more salon clients without paid ads?

Complete profile plus honest reviews plus visible work. Post real before-and-afters with the service and city named, ask every client for a review at the mirror moment, and make booking one tap away. My take: that free loop is the place to start before you spend on ads.

What should a salon post on social media?

Your work, mostly: transformations, before-and-afters, process clips, with client permission. Add the people behind the chairs and honest answers to common questions (pricing, upkeep, booking). Skip generic beauty quotes; they could be any salon, which is the problem.

Do salons need their own website?

My take: a simple one, yes. Services with prices, photos of your work, a booking link, and clear text saying what you do and where. Instagram alone gives Google and AI apps less to read about you, and you're one platform change away from losing reach.

Are paid ads worth it for a salon?

My take: after the foundation, with a specific offer, they can be worth testing at a small budget, like a new-client special on Instagram where the work sells itself. Before the foundation, no: an ad pointing at an empty profile pays for clicks that go nowhere.
