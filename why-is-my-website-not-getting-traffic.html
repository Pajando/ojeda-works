Website up, but the phone stays quiet? Why your site isn't getting traffic, and the fix.

Your site is live, but the phone doesn't ring from it. Hard truth: a website isn't owed traffic. Search engines send people to pages that answer what they typed. My take: a lot of small-business sites don't answer anything a customer would type. They say “Welcome to our website” six different ways and wonder why strangers never show up. Here are the three things to check, in order.

Layer one: can Google even read it?

Google's Search Essentials lists the bare minimum: Google's crawler isn't blocked, the page works, and the page has text Google can index. Common ways small-business sites trip: the site is one big image, or a script that shows no text. The key info lives only in Instagram posts. Page titles just say “Home.” There's no sitemap. Or it loads so slowly on a phone that people leave first. Free check: search Google for site:yourdomain.com. Google's SEO Starter Guide suggests the same check. If your pages aren't there, or the titles mean nothing, start here. Nothing else matters until Google can read you.

Layer two: does any page answer a real question?

Traffic comes from searches. Pages called “Home,” “About” and “Services” don't answer anything a stranger types. People search “how much does a kitchen repaint cost” or “dog groomer in [town] prices.” The site that answers those in plain text has a shot at those visitors. Google's SEO Starter Guide puts it this way: “Think about the words that a user might search for to find a piece of your content.” So the plan is simple: one page for each real question your customers ask, written honestly.A page that answers a question nobody asks won't bring anyone in. My take: this is the layer small sites miss most, and it's the easiest one to fix.

Layer three: does anyone vouch for you?

Say two sites both answer the question. Which one shows up? For local results, Google says part of it is how well-known you are, “based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have” (Google Business Profile Help). So: a complete business profile, the same name, address and phone everywhere, real reviews, and links from local groups, the chamber, suppliers and the local paper. Not bought links. Real ones, earned the slow way. Google also says: “There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google.” My take: AI assistants seem to lean on the same signals when someone asks for a recommendation, so this work pays off twice. It's real work. You mostly do it once, then keep it up. It's the work we do.

Questions people actually ask

How long does it take for a new website to get traffic?

Longer than you'd like. Google's SEO Starter Guide says some changes show up in a few hours and others take several months, and it suggests waiting a few weeks before you judge your work. My take: a new site with no pages that answer real questions can stay near zero for a long time. That's the real problem to fix.

Why does my website show up for my business name but nothing else?

Because your name is the only thing it clearly answers. Showing up for your own name is the easy part. Showing up for “what you sell + your town” takes pages that answer those searches in plain text. Give each real service its own useful page. Don't make near-copy pages for every nearby town. Google's spam policies call that doorway abuse.

Do I need to blog every week to get traffic?

No. How often you post matters less than whether the page is useful. My take: one page that fully answers a question people really search beats a pile of weekly posts written to fill a calendar. Check that the question is real, answer it better than what shows up now, and keep it up to date.

Is social media traffic a substitute for search traffic?

No. They do different jobs. Social reaches people who weren't looking. Search catches people right when they need you. My take: if you have followers but nobody can find you on Google, you're missing the people who are ready to buy today.
