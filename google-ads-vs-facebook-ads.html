Advertising

Paid for ads and got no customers? Google Ads vs Facebook Ads, honestly.

You paid for ads and the phone stayed quiet. My take: sometimes the ad did its job, just not the job you needed. Google shows ads to people searching for what you do. Facebook and Instagram show ads to people who fit a profile but weren't searching. Here's how to pick.

Intent versus interest

Google Ads catch people who are already looking, like “emergency plumber napa” or “tacos near me.” Google pitches it as getting in front of customers “when they're searching for businesses like yours” (Google Ads Help). The person has the problem now, and you're paying to be the answer. My take: clicks can cost more in busy fields like legal or plumbing, but the person clicking was looking for what you sell.

Facebook and Instagram ads reach people who fit a profile but weren't looking. Meta lets you aim by location, age and interests (Meta: ad targeting). My take: the clicks are often cheaper, but turning them into customers takes longer, because you're starting the conversation, not finishing it.

The honest defaults by business type

My take, as a starting point. Urgent jobs (repair, medical, legal): Google first, because the person needs help now. Things people want when they see them (food, retail, events, beauty): Facebook and Instagram first, because the photo makes them want it. Big decisions (contractors, high-ticket services): often both. Google catches the searchers, and social keeps you in front of them while they decide.

A test that beats any rule: ask your last ten customers how they found you. Spend where the answers point. That's a channel already working for your business, not someone else's.

What matters more than the platform

My take: the offer and the page the ad sends people to matter more than the platform. One specific, true offer, sent to a page that answers the question the ad raised, and tracked by what each real customer costs you. That setup gives you a fair test on either platform. “Check us out” sent to a slow homepage is a weak test on both.

Quick answers

Which is cheaper, Google Ads or Facebook Ads?

My take: Facebook clicks are often cheaper than Google clicks in the same field. But cheap clicks from people who weren't looking can cost more per real customer than pricey clicks from people who were. Judge by what each new customer costs you, not what each click costs. The two platforms sell different things.

Can I run both Google and Facebook ads at once?

You can. My take: a small budget split two ways often teaches you little about either. Test one platform first with a budget you can afford to lose, learn what a customer costs, then add the second. One at a time until you have enough money to measure both.

Why did my Facebook ads get clicks but no customers?

My take: clicks were the wrong goal. People who weren't searching tend to browse. The fix is usually one of three: a more specific offer, a page that gives them a clear next step, or admitting your customers search when they need you, which points to Google. Those people were browsing, not buying.

Do I need ads at all if my business shows up in search?

Maybe not. A Google Business Profile is free, and Google says complete, accurate info makes you more likely to show up in local results (Google Business Profile Help). Add a clear website. My take: ads earn their cost when you need more customers than free search brings, or you need them now. Fix the free stuff first, then pay to promote what already works.
