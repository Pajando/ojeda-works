Scared Google Ads will eat your money while the phone stays quiet? Here's what they really cost.

The fear is real: you pay, the clicks come, and the phone stays quiet. Here's the good news. You pick the budget. Google's own help page says “There's no minimum spending commitment, and you set and control your own budget.” My take: what a click costs depends on your trade and your town. Below: how to get Google's estimate of that price before your ads run, and the math that tells you if ads will pay.

What a click typically costs

Google says “Google Ads determines which ads should show with a lightning-fast ad auction”. You bid on keywords, like “plumber Napa,” against everyone else who wants them. My take: that's why the price of a click changes by trade and by city. I won't give you a national average here, because your town's number is the only one that matters. You can look it up. Google calls its Keyword Planner “a free resource to help advertisers build keyword lists for search campaigns.” It lets you “View the average cost for your ad to show on searches for a keyword.” One catch: to open it, you have to set up a Google Ads account, add billing info, and create a campaign first. And the click price alone tells you nothing. What matters is how many clicks turn into customers.

The budget math that actually matters

Work backwards from one customer. Made-up example: say clicks cost $5 and one visitor in twenty calls you. Then each call costs you $100 (that's $5 times 20 clicks). Not every call books a job, so each customer costs you more than that. That's still great if a customer is worth $2,000. It's brutal if they're worth $150. That's the whole game: know your click price, how many clicks turn into calls, and what a customer is worth. Then the right budget falls out of the math. One thing to know before you set it. For regular click campaigns, Google “will allow up to twice your average daily budget” on a busy day, but over the month you won't be charged more than your average daily budget times the average number of days in a month (30.4). My take: if your budget can't buy enough clicks to expect a few calls a month, you won't learn anything. You're guessing with extra steps. Local Services Ads work differently. You don't pay per click. Google says “You only pay when potential customers get in touch through your ads on Local Services Ads.” Google doesn't list a set price per lead on that page, so I won't guess one.

When Google Ads are the wrong answer

My take: ads make a busy business busier. They don't fix a weak one. If your Google Business Profile is thin, your reviews are few, or your website doesn't get people to call, paid clicks land on that weakness and the money is gone. Fix the free stuff first. Showing up in regular Google results and in AI answers costs nothing per click. Ads make sense after that, to reach people who don't know your name yet. And if you run them, keep it tight: the exact towns you serve, a short list of keywords, and tracking all the way to the booked job. Never “boost” and hope.

Questions people actually ask

What is the minimum budget for Google Ads?

There isn't one. Google says “There's no minimum spending commitment, and you set and control your own budget.” My take: a tiny budget is safe, but it may not buy enough clicks to tell you anything. Do the math for your trade first.

How much do Google Ads cost per click?

It's an auction, so my take is the price changes by trade and by city. I won't quote a national average, because your town's price is the one that counts. Google calls its Keyword Planner “a free resource” for building keyword lists, and it shows the average cost for your exact keywords. To open it, you need a Google Ads account and a campaign set up.

Are Google Ads worth it for a small business?

My take: only when the math works. Take what a click costs and multiply it by how many clicks it takes to get one customer. That number has to be well under what a customer is worth. And the profile and website those clicks land on have to turn visitors into calls. Fix the free stuff first. Ads pointed at a weak profile just spend faster.

What are Local Services Ads and what do they cost?

Local Services Ads are a Google ad type for local service businesses, like home, health, beauty and auto services. You don't pay per click. Google says “You only pay when potential customers get in touch through your ads on Local Services Ads.” Google doesn't list a set price per lead on that page, so I won't guess one. My take: it fits urgent jobs, like plumbers and electricians, if your reviews are strong.
