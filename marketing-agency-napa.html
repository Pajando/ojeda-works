Paying for marketing and the phone still isn't ringing? Read this before you hire a marketing agency in Napa.

You pay for marketing every month, and the phone isn't ringing any more than before. Before you sign another agency contract, know what you're buying. There's also another way to get the work done: one person, with no account manager between you and the work.

What an agency retainer actually buys

An agency usually bundles strategy, content, ads and reports into one monthly fee. Some of that money pays for real skill. My take: some of it also pays for the setup around the skill. That means account managers who pass messages between you and the people doing the work, meetings, and case studies from businesses that aren't like yours.

That doesn't make agencies bad. My take: for a business with a big budget and nonstop campaigns, the setup can be worth it. For a Napa small business that needs a website people can find, a video that says something true, and pages that answer real customer questions, a lot of that setup is cost you may not need.

The questions that cut through any pitch

Ask who will actually do your work. Not who's in the meeting, who does the work. Ask what will change about how customers find you, in plain words. Ask what happens to your accounts, your website and your content if you leave. Ask what they think one new customer will cost you. My take: if they can't answer, they're selling activity, not results. And if they promise rankings, remember Google's own words: “There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google” (Google Business Profile Help).

Questions people actually ask

How much does a marketing agency cost in Napa?

It varies a lot with what's included, so get two or three quotes in writing and compare them line by line. My take: don't look only at the monthly fee. Ask how much of it pays for people doing your work versus the setup around them, and what one new customer ends up costing you.

What's the difference between an agency and a freelancer?

An agency usually gives you a team, a process and more hands, often with an account manager between you and the work. A freelancer or one-person shop gives you direct access to the person doing the work. My take: you'll find good and bad work on both sides. You're mostly choosing the setup.

Do Napa businesses really need marketing help?

My take: many need less than they're sold. Some of the most useful work is free: a complete Google Business Profile, a website that answers real customer questions, and honest reviews. Paid help is worth it when it builds those basics or adds to them, not when it's posting just to post.

Why does being recommended by AI matter for a Napa business?

My take: some customers now ask ChatGPT or Google's AI for a recommendation instead of scrolling the results. Those tools work from what's written about you online, so your website, profile and reviews should say clearly what you do and where.
