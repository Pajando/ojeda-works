Looking for a marketing agency in Napa? Read this first.

Before you sign an agency retainer, it's worth knowing what you're actually buying — and that there's a different way to get the same work done, without the twelve-person roster or the account manager between you and the person doing it.

What an agency retainer actually buys

A typical agency arrangement bundles strategy, content, ads, and reporting into a monthly retainer. Some of that money buys real expertise. A real share of it buys the structure around the expertise: account managers who translate between you and the people doing the work, meetings about the work instead of the work, and case studies from clients whose situation isn't yours.

None of that makes agencies bad — for businesses with big budgets and constant campaigns, the structure earns its keep. For a Napa small business that needs a website that gets found, video that says something true, and content that answers real customer questions, the structure is mostly overhead you're financing.

The questions that cut through any pitch

Ask who will actually do your work — not who's in the meeting, who does the work. Ask what will change about how customers find you, specifically. Ask what happens to your accounts, your site, and your content if you leave. And ask what one customer costs to acquire through what they're proposing — if they can't frame an answer, they're selling activity, not results.

Questions people actually ask

How much does a marketing agency cost in Napa?

Small-business agency retainers commonly run $1,000–$5,000+ a month depending on scope. The number to interrogate isn't the retainer — it's what share pays for people doing your work versus the structure around them, and what one acquired customer actually costs you through their efforts.

What's the difference between an agency and a freelancer?

An agency gives you a team, process, and capacity — with an account-manager layer between you and the work. A freelancer or one-person shop gives you direct access to the person doing the work, usually at a fraction of the overhead. Quality exists and varies wildly in both; the structure is what you're choosing between.

Do Napa businesses really need marketing help?

Many need less than they're sold. The highest-return work is often free: a complete Google Business Profile, a website that answers real customer questions, and honest reviews. Paid help earns its cost when it's building those foundations or amplifying them — not when it's posting for the sake of posting.

Why does being recommended by AI matter for a Napa business?

Customers increasingly ask ChatGPT and Google's AI for recommendations instead of scrolling results. Those systems recommend businesses they can read and verify. That's the visibility layer most marketing plans skip — and checking it is exactly what AO Audit was built to do.
