Google Business Profile · From my own business

Changing your business name? On Google, the wrong kind of change means starting over, and your reviews stay behind.

Most owners think a new name is one edit on Google. Sometimes it is. But Google's own rules say a bigger change makes you a new business: you close the old profile and start a new one. The reviews you worked for stay on the closed one. I'm in that exact spot right now, so I read the rules before touching anything.

By Alejandro Ojeda, Napa, CA · October 23, 2026

My situation

My Google profile is still under my first business name, The AO Audit. Since then everything I do moved under one name: Ojeda Works. The website says Ojeda Works. My email says Ojeda Works. The Google profile doesn't.

The easy move is to open the profile, type the new name and hit Save. Before doing that, on October 23, 2026, I read five of Google's own help pages on names, reviews, editing, verifying and closing. Here's what they say, in Google's words.

The rule: small change or new business

Google calls an allowed rename "rebranding." It counts if the change is small (or if a business with several locations renames them all):

"a minor name change, in which the proper nouns and services described in the business name remain unchanged and the business category remains unchanged."

Anything bigger, Google says, "it's considered a new business." And then:

"You must mark the existing Business Profile as closed and then create a new Business Profile with your new business name."

Source: Guidelines for representing your business on Google, read October 23, 2026.

What counts as "minor"?

Google gives the test but no examples. Here's how I read it. These are made-up examples, and the reading is mine, not Google's.

Old nameNew nameMy reading

Rosa's TacosValley TacosNew business. The proper noun changed.
Rosa's TacosRosa's CateringNew business. The service in the name changed, and likely the category too.

What happens to your reviews

Google's reviews page spells it out:

  • Minor name change: "the reviews remain."
  • Bigger change: "it's considered a new business."
  • Closed profile: "the reviews remain on the profile." But customers can leave new reviews "for temporarily closed businesses but not for permanently closed ones."

Google does offer to move reviews between profiles in some cases. The cases it lists: a duplicate profile, and a move to a new address with the same name. (A new owner who keeps the name doesn't need a move. The reviews just stay.) A full name change isn't on that list. Google also says "Not all business changes qualify to move or remove reviews."

Source: Google Business Profile Help, reviews when your business changes, read October 23, 2026.

So for a big rename, the new profile starts at zero reviews. The old ones sit on a closed profile that can't get any more.

"Can't I just keep both profiles?"

I asked myself that too. Google says: "There should only be one profile per business." Two brands at one spot can each have a profile only if they run independently. My take: The AO Audit is one service inside a one-person business, so it doesn't qualify for its own profile. If yours is a real, separate business with its own name, staff and category, read the guidelines yourself before deciding.

How to do it, step by step

  1. Get your proof ready first. Google wants the name on your profile "exactly as it appears on your real world business signage, stationery, and other branding." So the new name should already be on your sign, your website, your cards or invoices. (Edit your Business Profile)
  2. Minor change? Go to your Business Profile, select "Edit profile," change the name, then "Save." Google warns: "you might need to verify your business again."
  3. Bigger change? Mark the old profile closed: "Edit profile," then "Hours," then "Edit" next to Hours, then "Permanently closed," then "Save." (Mark your business as closed) Then create the new profile under the new name and verify it.
  4. Don't touch the name while a postcard code is on the way. Google: "Do not edit your business name, address, or category on your Business Profile." If you do, "the code we sent you won't work." (Verify your business)
  5. Ask for reviews again. Your new profile starts empty. Your best customers are the ones to ask first. Here's how to ask without it being awkward.

What I'm doing

My take: I'd rather lose 3 reviews than keep a name on Google that doesn't match my sign, my website or my email. And I'd rather follow Google's rule than type a new name into the old profile and hope nobody notices. A profile Google trusts is worth more than 3 reviews.

The real risk, the way I see it: I close the old profile and the new one gets stuck in verification. So my proof goes in place first, then the switch.

What this page can't tell you

  • I'm not Google. I'm quoting Google's help pages as they read on October 23, 2026. They carry no date and can change. Click the links and check before you act.
  • "Minor" is a judgment call. My table is how I read Google's test. Google support has the last word on your case.
  • It's one business, mine, and I haven't finished the switch yet. I can't tell you yet how long verification took or whether anything went wrong.
  • Google doesn't say what happens if you rename the wrong way. I didn't find a page that spells out a penalty, so I'm not going to guess one. I'm just following the rule as written.
