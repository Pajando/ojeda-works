Foundation

Locals look you up on Facebook and find nothing? Make a Facebook page for your business.

Someone hears about you and looks you up on Facebook. They should find your hours, your phone number and a way to message you. Making the page is quick. Making it work takes the right settings and knowing what a page can't do. Both are below.

The setup, correctly

Facebook says anyone with a Facebook account can create a Page (Facebook Help). Log in, click Pages, then Create Page. Use the business name exactly as customers know it, the same as on your Google profile and website. Pick the closest real category, then add your contact info, location and hours. Add a profile photo (your logo works) and a cover photo of your actual place or work, and set the action button. My take: when your name matches everywhere, people and search tools can tell it's the same business.

The settings that actually matter

Turn on messaging and set an automatic reply that says when you'll really answer. A message nobody answers is a customer nobody served. Set the action button (call, book, order) to match how you actually take business. Make sure the page is public. And add a second person you trust to help manage it. My take: a page only one person can get into is one forgotten password away from trouble.

What a page honestly can and can't do

A page gives you a place on Facebook where customers can find you and message you. What it can't do is replace a website. You don't decide who sees your posts. Meta says what people see in Facebook Feed is “selected, ranked and delivered” by an AI system (Meta Transparency Center). And it's Facebook's platform, not yours. My take: use the page for being seen and talking with customers, and a website you own for being found. Each one does its own job.

Quick answers

Do I need a personal Facebook account to make a business page?

You need a Facebook account. Facebook says anyone with one can create a Page or help manage one. Don't make a second, fake personal account for the business. Facebook's terms say to “create only one account (your own) and use it for personal purposes” (Facebook Terms). Add a trusted second person to help manage the page instead.

Should my business use a Facebook page or a personal profile?

A page. Facebook's terms say to use your account for personal purposes (Facebook Terms). My take: running your business from a personal profile puts the account you depend on at risk. A page keeps the business separate from your personal life.

Is a Facebook page enough, or do I need a website too?

My take: a page alone isn't enough. You don't control who sees your posts, and it's Facebook's platform, not yours. A simple website you own is the base, and the page is one channel. You want both, doing different jobs.

How often should I post on my business page?

My take: post enough to look open and alive. A couple of real posts a week beats daily filler. The bigger jobs are correct info, answered messages and reviews. A page with current hours and fast replies does more for you than a busy page where nobody answers.
