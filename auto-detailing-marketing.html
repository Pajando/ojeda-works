Content & Social

Calendar full one week, empty the next? Auto detailing marketing, the honest way.

My take: few trades get before-and-afters this good. A disaster interior turned showroom-clean does a lot of the selling for you. The marketing job is aiming that content at the right buyers: the one-time deep clean, and the monthly account that actually builds the business.

Transformations do the selling

Shoot every dramatic job: the dog-hair disaster, the spilled-milkshake resurrection, the paint correction under shop lights. Before and after from the same angle, no filter games. The work is dramatic enough. Post to Instagram and TikTok, and load the Google Business Profile with the same shots. Caption with the service name and town, the same words a customer would type, like 'interior detail [city]'.

Chase the recurring account, not just the rescue job

One-time deep cleans pay the bills; monthly maintenance accounts build a business. Pitch the next step out loud: full detail now, maintenance plan after, so the car stays like this without another rescue job. Fleet and dealership relationships are the same play at scale: one account can mean a regular weekly route. Every rescue-job customer is a maintenance-plan prospect, so ask every one of them.

The foundation and what to skip

Profile complete with service menu and honest pricing ranges. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results. People want to know what a full detail costs, so answer it in plain text. State clearly whether you're mobile, shop-based, or both, and your actual service area. What to skip: competing with the car wash on price. You sell hours of skilled labor, and the photos show it. My take: skip Groupon-style blasts too, since they tend to fill the calendar with one-time bargain hunters.

Questions people actually ask

How do auto detailers get more customers?

Before-and-after content posted relentlessly, a complete profile with prices and service area, and reviews mentioning specific jobs. My take: the work photographs so well that steady posting is worth more than paid ads at the start.

How much should a detailer charge?

Price by hours and condition, not against car washes: set your own ranges and show them. List what moves the number, like pet hair or stains, so the price talk happens before the job, not in the driveway.

Do monthly maintenance plans work for detailing?

My take: yes. They turn a one-time rescue into repeat work and help fill the slow weeks. Pitch at handoff, when the owner is staring at the transformation. Fleets and dealerships are the same logic at contract scale.

Should a detailer be mobile or have a shop?

Marketing-wise, say which you are everywhere, because it's one of the first things a customer needs to know. My take: mobile sells convenience, and a shop is easier to sell for corrections and ceramic work. Either way, the service-area and setup answer belongs on the site in plain text.
