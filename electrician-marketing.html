Electrician marketing: the honest playbook.

Electrical work splits into two businesses — the emergency call and the planned project — and they're won differently. Most electrician marketing advice misses that split. Here's the honest version.

Two businesses, two plays

Emergency work (“electrician near me”, dead panel, sparking outlet) is won at the search moment: complete profile, real reviews, fast answer, service area clear. Project work (panel upgrades, remodels, EV chargers, generators) is researched: photos of finished installs, a page per major service in plain text, and reviews naming the job type. The EV-charger and generator pages deserve special attention — growing searches, planned purchases, healthy tickets.

The license is a marketing asset

Homeowners fear electrical work going wrong more than any trade — licensed, bonded, insured stated plainly, license number visible, permits pulled properly and said so. The trust layer converts before price is discussed: clean photos of tidy panel work signal the professionalism words claim. Ask for reviews at final walkthrough, in the customer's words, naming the job and town — “rewired our 1960s house in [town]” wins you the next one.

Leads and ads, with the math

Search ads convert on urgent terms — start small, cap by service area, track cost per booked job. Lead platforms sell shared electrical leads at meaningful prices: run the honest math on closed jobs, not calls. And the boring winner: being findable and readable to search engines and AI assistants for free — the foundation most competitors leave half-built.

Questions people actually ask

How do electricians get more residential customers?

Win the urgent search with a complete, review-rich profile; win project work with service pages and install photos that prove the craft. The two feed each other — the emergency fix done right becomes the panel-upgrade quote next year.

Are lead generation sites worth it for electricians?

Measure before believing: shared leads mean racing other electricians to a phone call, and per-lead prices add up fast. Track cost per closed job versus what your own profile and referrals produce — established electricians usually find their own presence cheaper.

What should an electrician put on their website?

A page per major service (panel, rewiring, EV charging, generators, troubleshooting) in plain text with the service area, license info, real install photos, and reviews. Machines recommending electricians can only read text — the site is what makes you recommendable.

How do electricians compete with big franchise companies?

On proof and person: the franchise has ad budget, you have the actual electrician's name, photos, and neighbors' reviews. Complete local foundation plus honest reviews consistently outranks generic franchise pages in local results — the fight is winnable where you actually live.
