Advertising

Phone quiet between jobs? Electrician marketing that brings the right calls honestly.

The phone rings for small fixes, but the big panel and EV jobs go to someone else? My take: electrical work is two businesses, the emergency call and the planned project, and you win them in different ways.

Two businesses, two plays

Emergency work (“electrician near me”, dead panel, sparking outlet) is won at the search moment: complete profile, real reviews, fast answer, service area clear. Project work (panel upgrades, remodels, EV chargers, generators) gets researched: photos of finished installs, a page per major service in plain text, and honest reviews from real jobs. My take: EV-charger and generator pages are worth extra care, because people plan those purchases and compare before they call.

The license is a marketing asset

Homeowners letting someone work on their wiring want proof first. Put your license number where people can see it and check it, say plainly whether you're bonded and insured (only if you are), and say that you pull permits when the job needs one. My take: clean photos of tidy panel work show your standards before price ever comes up. At the final walkthrough, ask every customer for an honest review. Don't tell them what to write, and don't ask only the happy ones. Google's review rules ban asking only happy customers.

Leads and ads, with the math

My take: search ads make the most sense on urgent searches. Start small, limit them to your service area, and track cost per booked job. Some lead sites sell the same lead to several electricians, so do the math on closed jobs, not calls. And the boring one: a complete, accurate free profile. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results.

Questions people actually ask

How do electricians get more residential customers?

Win the urgent search with a complete, review-rich profile; win project work with service pages and install photos that prove the craft. My take: the two feed each other, and an emergency fix done right can turn into a panel-upgrade quote later.

Are lead generation sites worth it for electricians?

Measure before you believe. When a lead is shared, you race other electricians to the phone, and per-lead prices add up. Track cost per closed job against what your own profile and referrals bring in. My take: once you're established, your own profile is often the cheaper source.

What should an electrician put on their website?

A page per major service (panel, rewiring, EV charging, generators, troubleshooting) in plain text with the service area, license info, real install photos, and reviews. My take: search engines and AI tools mostly read text, so plain words on the page help them understand what you do and where.

How do electricians compete with big franchise companies?

On proof and person: the franchise has ad budget, you have the actual electrician's name, photos, and neighbors' reviews. Nobody can promise a ranking; Google says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. My take: a complete profile and honest reviews give a local electrician a fair shot against the big names in their own town.
