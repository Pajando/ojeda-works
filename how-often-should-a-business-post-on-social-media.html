Posting and nobody sees it? How often a business should post on social media

You post, almost nobody sees it, and the phone stays quiet. My take: there is no magic number. Post as often as you can keep up without posting filler. One real post beats five filler posts.

Why the magic numbers are mostly noise

My take: most “post this many times a week” charts are made with big brands in mind, not a one-person shop. For you, the real limit is supply: how many posts can you make that actually say something? Instagram says one thing it uses to rank your feed is your history of interacting with someone, like whether you comment on each other's posts. My take: quote graphics and holiday clip-art give people no reason to comment. Real work does.

A rhythm you can keep up

My take: two to four posts a week is plenty for most small businesses. That's enough to stay in mind, and few enough that every post can earn its place. The mix that holds up: proof of work (the thing you made or fixed today), answers to real customer questions, and now and then a human moment. One owner can keep that up, and that matters more than any chart. I'd rather see two posts a week, every week, than a burst of posts and then a month of nothing.

The order of operations, honestly

My take: how often you post is a second-floor question. If your Google Business Profile is missing info, or your website doesn't answer basic customer questions, fix those first. Google says that to help it “match it to relevant searches,” you should provide complete and detailed business info. My take: social keeps you warm with people who already found you. It rarely does the finding.

Questions people actually ask

Is it bad to post too much on social media?

My take: posting a lot isn't the problem. Filler is. If you have something real to show every day, post every day. If your posts get worse as you post more, you're posting more than you have to say. Slow down.

What happens if my business stops posting for a while?

My take: you can come back from a break. Just start posting real work again. The bigger cost is with people: a page that's been quiet for months can make a customer wonder if you're still open. A light rhythm you can keep up helps you avoid that.

What should a small business actually post?

My take: post proof of work. The job finished, the dish plated, the before-and-after. Answers to questions customers really ask. Now and then, the person behind the business. Skip inspirational quotes and generic holiday graphics. Any business could post those, and that's the problem.

Do I need to be on every platform?

My take: no. One platform done well beats four done halfway. Ask your last ten customers where they spend time, pick that one, and own it. On the others, keep a complete profile that points to the one you use.
