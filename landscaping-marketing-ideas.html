Advertising

Gaps in your route and a quiet phone after the season? Landscaping marketing ideas that fill them, street by street.

Your crew drives across town between jobs, and the phone goes quiet when the season ends. My take: landscaping has two things going for it. Your work is easy to photograph, and the neighbors see it every day. Here's how to use both.

Your work is the ad

Take a before photo and an after photo of every job, from the same spot. It takes a minute with your phone. Post them with the town named, and add them to your Google profile. My take: a profile full of local before-and-afters does more selling than ad copy can, because the photos are the proof.

Win streets, not scattered jobs

My take: your next customer may live next door to your current one. The neighbors walk past your work every day. So work the street. Leave a simple “we service this street” door hanger with the neighbors after a visit, thank your current customer with a referral perk, and put a yard sign out while you work. Check your city and HOA rules on signs and door hangers first. More jobs on one street also means less drive time between stops.

Time the seasons like a calendar business

My take: landscaping work follows the calendar. Spring cleanups get booked in late winter, irrigation in early summer, leaf work in fall, and next year's contracts before this season ends. So post each service a season early, on your website and Google profile, in plain text. Then it's already there when people start searching. Offering yearly contracts in the fall can help with the slow winter months.

Questions people actually ask

How do landscapers get customers fast in spring?

Get seen before the rush. Have your cleanup offers on your website and Google profile by late winter, post your before-and-afters, and send past customers an email or postcard with an early-booking price. My take: the crews who show up early get the first calls.

Do yard signs and door hangers still work?

My take: for this trade, yes. People see your work right where they live. A sign during the job and a door hanger for the neighbors after it reach the people who just watched your crew work. They're cheap and local. Check your city and HOA rules first.

What should a landscaping company post on social media?

Before-and-afters, time-lapses of a yard coming together, tips for your local climate each season, and your crew at work. Name the town in captions. Skip stock lawn photos. My take: your posts matter because they're real and they're local.

Is it worth advertising lawn care on Google?

My take: it can be, in your busy season, because someone searching “lawn care near me” usually wants help. Start small, send ads to a page with photos and prices, and track what each booked job costs. Fix your Google profile and photos first. Without proof on the page, you pay for clicks from people who leave.
