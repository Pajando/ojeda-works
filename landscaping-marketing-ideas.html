Advertising

Landscaping marketing ideas that win whole neighborhoods.

Landscaping is the most photographable trade there is, and its customers cluster on the same streets. Those two facts are the whole marketing strategy. Here's how to use them.

Your work is the ad

Every job produces the most persuasive content in home services: the before-and-after. Shoot both, same angle, every job — thirty seconds with a phone. Post them with the town named; load them to your Google profile by the dozen. A profile full of local transformations converts searchers that no ad copy can, because the proof *is* the pitch.

Win streets, not scattered jobs

Your next best customer lives next door to your current one — they see the truck weekly and the lawn daily. Work the cluster: a simple “we service this street” door hanger on the neighbors after each visit, a referral perk for the current customer, yard signage while you work. Route density also makes every job more profitable — marketing and operations pointing the same direction.

Time the seasons like a calendar business

Landscaping demand is a schedule: spring cleanups sell in late winter, irrigation in early summer, leaf work in fall, and next season's contracts before this one ends. Publish each service a season early — on the site and profile, in text machines can read — so you're the answer when the search happens, not after. Annual-contract offers in fall smooth the winter cliff.

Questions people actually ask

How do landscapers get customers fast in spring?

The fast lever is being visible before the rush: cleanup offers live on your site and profile by late winter, before-and-afters posted, and an email or postcard to past customers with early-bird pricing. Spring demand is real; the winners just showed up before it hit.

Do yard signs and door hangers still work?

For this trade specifically, yes — your buyers are literally standing on the evidence. A sign during the job and a neighbor door-hanger after it target the exact people who watched your work happen. Cheap, local, and better targeted than most digital spend.

What should a landscaping company post on social media?

Before-and-afters, time-lapses of transformations, seasonal tips for your climate, and the crew being human. Name the town in captions. Skip stock lawn photos — the entire value of your content is that it's real and provably local.

Is it worth advertising lawn care on Google?

In competitive seasons, yes — searches like “lawn care near me” carry real intent. Start small, point ads at a page with proof and prices, and measure cost per booked job. But the profile-and-photos foundation converts those clicks; without it, ads pay for lookers who leave.
