Real estate marketing ideas that win listings.

Listings expire; the agent brand doesn't. The agents who win market themselves as the neighborhood's trusted answer — and increasingly, the answer AI gives when someone asks. Here's the honest playbook.

Build the asset that doesn't expire

Your marketing spine is you: what you know about your market, what you're like to work with, proof of closings. Market-knowledge content works hardest — short honest answers to what buyers and sellers in your area actually ask (what's happening with prices here, what does escrow really look like, is now a bad time). It proves expertise instead of claiming it, and it's exactly what search engines and AI assistants surface when someone asks about your market.

Social ideas that actually work (and open houses)

Post ideas with legs: just-listed and just-sold with the story behind each, neighborhood spotlights (the coffee place, the school run, the street nobody knows), honest market updates in plain words, client key-day moments with permission, and behind-the-scenes of the unglamorous work. Open houses double as content and lead capture: promote to neighbors (sellers-to-be live next door), make sign-in worth it, and follow up same-day — the visit was the easy part.

Video, and the check most agents skip

Video converts trust faster than any medium in this trade — listing tours sell the property, but the agent video and market updates sell *you*. And the layer almost no agent checks: when someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI for an agent in your area, are you the answer? Questions people actually ask

What's the best marketing for a new listing?

Great photos plus a vertical video clip posted where locals scroll, the just-listed story on your profiles, and neighbor outreach — the most likely next seller watches from next door. The listing markets you as much as the property; treat every one as a brand asset.

What should realtors post on social media?

Market knowledge in plain words, neighborhood life, listing stories, closing celebrations with permission, and the honest behind-the-scenes. The test: does this prove I know this market and I'm good to work with? Inspirational quotes prove neither.

Do open houses still work?

As listing-selling events, sometimes; as agent-marketing events, reliably — neighbors and early-stage buyers walk in and meet you. Work the follow-up same day and the open house pays even when the buyer comes from elsewhere.

How do realtors show up in AI recommendations?

AI assistants recommend agents they can read and verify: complete profiles, consistent info, real reviews, and content proving local expertise. Most agents have none of it structured; checking and fixing that is exactly what AO Audit's realtor program does.
