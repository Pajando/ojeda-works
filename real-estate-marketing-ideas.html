Sellers listing with someone else? Real estate marketing ideas that earn the next listing, honestly.

A neighbor sells, and they call another agent. You didn't even know they were thinking about it. Listings come and go, but your name stays in the neighborhood. Here's how to build it, honestly.

Build the asset that doesn't expire

What you're really marketing is you: what you know about your market, what you're like to work with, and proof of your closings. My take: local know-how is the strongest thing you can post. Write short, honest answers to what buyers and sellers in your area actually ask, like what prices are doing here, what escrow really looks like, or whether now is a bad time. That shows you know the market instead of just saying it. Put those answers on your site as plain text too. Google says its AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special tricks, and its list of basic practices includes making important content available as text (Google Search Central).

Social ideas worth trying (and open houses)

Post ideas I'd start with: just-listed and just-sold posts with the story behind each home, neighborhood spotlights (the coffee place, the park, the street nobody knows), honest market updates in plain words, closing-day moments with the client's permission, and the unglamorous work behind a sale. Describe the home and the place, not who you think should live there. Open houses give you content and new contacts: invite the neighbors, give people a reason to sign in, and follow up the same day. The visit was the easy part.

Video, and a question to ask about AI

My take: a listing tour sells the house, but a short video of you talking about your market sells you. Here's a question worth asking: when someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI for an agent in your town, does your name come up? Try it yourself. If you don't show up, make sure your profiles are complete, your info matches everywhere, and your market answers are written on your site where anyone can read them. Questions people actually ask

What's the best marketing for a new listing?

Great photos, a short vertical video for social, the just-listed story on your profiles, and a note to the neighbors. My take: every listing markets you as much as the home, so treat each one like an ad for your work.

What should realtors post on social media?

Market knowledge in plain words, neighborhood life, listing stories, closing celebrations with permission, and the honest behind-the-scenes. The test: does this prove I know this market and I'm good to work with? Inspirational quotes prove neither.

Do open houses still work?

Sometimes, for selling that home. They're also a chance to meet neighbors and early buyers face to face. Follow up the same day, and the open house can still pay off even if the buyer comes from somewhere else.

How do realtors show up in AI recommendations?

No one can promise you'll show up. Google says its AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special optimization, and its basic practices include making important content available in text form and keeping your Business Profile info up to date. So start there: complete profiles, the same info everywhere, real reviews, and plain written answers about your local market.
