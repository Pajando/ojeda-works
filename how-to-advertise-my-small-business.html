Advertising

Ads running, phone still quiet? How to advertise my small business in the right order.

You paid for ads. People clicked. The phone stayed quiet. My take: that often happens when the ads go out before the basics are ready. Do the free work first, then put money behind one real offer. Here's the order.

Step one: be findable before you pay to be visible

Before you spend a dollar, claim and fill out the listings where people look you up: your Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and Yelp. Google says creating a Business Profile is free, and Yelp says claiming your Yelp page is free. My take: an ad that sends people to an empty profile burns money. They click, see nothing that convinces them, and leave.

Same for your website. If the page an ad lands on is slow, confusing, or doesn't answer what the ad promised, you're paying to send people into a leak. Fix the bucket before you buy more water.

Step two: pick one real offer

My take: ads that just say “check us out” don't give anyone a reason to call. Build the ad on one specific, true offer instead: a price, a guarantee, a first-visit deal, a real specialty. If you can't say the offer in one sentence, the ad isn't ready. Changing who sees it won't fix that.

Step three: put it where your customers actually are

Google search ads show up when someone searches for what you do, like a person with a leaky pipe looking for a plumber right now. Facebook and Instagram reach people by interest and location, so they suit work that looks good in a photo. Which one is right for you? Ask your last ten customers where they found you. My take: their answers matter more than any marketing guide, this one included.

Step four: measure one number, then scale or kill

Pick the one thing you're really paying for: a call, a booking, a sale. Track what each one costs you. Views and clicks can look great while the phone stays quiet, so don't judge an ad by them. Start small, watch that one number for a few weeks, then keep what works and stop what doesn't. My take: being willing to stop a bad ad fast saves more money than any settings trick.

Questions people actually ask

How can I advertise my small business for free?

Claim and fill out your Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and Yelp listings. Google says its Business Profile is free, and Yelp says claiming your page is free. Then ask happy customers for honest reviews. My take: do this before you pay for a single ad, so the people your ads send have something real to see.

How much should a small business spend on advertising?

My take: start small. Spend only enough to learn what one new customer costs you, then put more money only into what works. A set percent of sales sounds professional, but it doesn't tell you if the ads are working.

Should I advertise on Google or Facebook?

Google search ads show up when people search for what you do right now. Facebook and Instagram reach people by interest and location. My take: if people call you when something breaks, try Google first. If your work looks good in a photo, try Facebook and Instagram. Better yet, ask your last ten customers where they found you.

Why aren't my ads working?

My take: check three things first. Does the ad send people to a weak page or an empty profile? Is there a clear offer behind it? Are you tracking a real number, like calls or bookings? Fix those before you change who sees the ad.
