Advertising

Panicked customers calling someone else? Pest control marketing that earns the call and the plan, honestly.

Someone just saw a roach in the kitchen and called the first exterminator they found. It wasn't you. My take: a lot of pest control customers are in a hurry and a little embarrassed. Win that first call by being easy to find and easy to talk to, then turn the one-time fix into a plan. Here's how.

Respect the discretion, win the panic

My take: many customers don't want the neighbors to know. If you offer unmarked trucks or quiet visits, say so. Talk without judgment and skip the lectures. For the urgent call, the basics matter most: a complete Google profile with your service area and hours, recent reviews, a phone someone answers, and same-day service listed only if you really offer it. Google says complete and accurate info makes a business more likely to show up in local results, and more reviews and positive ratings can help (Google Business Profile Help).

The recurring plan is the business

My take: one-time treatments get you in the door. Prevention plans are what keep the business steady. Sell the plan as what it honestly is, the visit that keeps the fix fixed, with plain prices and easy scheduling. A plan customer is less likely to shop around at the next sighting, and regular stops on the same streets make your routes easier to plan.

Content that converts the squeamish

Write plain answers to the questions worried homeowners ask: “what does a termite look like,” “carpenter ants vs termites,” and what shows up in your area each season. Put them on your site as text. Google lists putting important content in text form among the basics for its AI search features (Google Search Central). Keep photos clear and helpful, not gross. You want to be the calm expert, not the shock video. When you ask for reviews, suggest customers mention the problem and the town in their own words. My take: a review like “ant problem gone in [town]” tells the next homeowner exactly what you fixed and where.

Questions people actually ask

How do pest control companies get new customers?

Be easy to find when it's urgent: a complete profile, recent reviews and a phone someone answers. Help early researchers with plain answers about which pest they have. Then offer a prevention plan after the first fix. My take: steady plan customers are what make a pest control business stable.

What should pest control companies post online?

Guides to tell pests apart, seasonal heads-ups for your area, prevention tips, and before-and-after results that don't show whose house it is. My take: helpful beats gross. A worried homeowner wants a calm expert, not a horror video. Tag the town and write it as text so search engines can read it.

Are quarterly pest plans easy to sell?

My take: easier when you're honest about it. It's prevention that keeps the fix fixed. A good time to offer it is right after a treatment works, when the customer is relieved. Contracts with hidden catches tend to cause cancellations and angry reviews. Plans that are easy to pause and show what you did each visit are easier to keep.

Does seasonal advertising work for pest control?

It can. My take: spend more when the pests in your area are busiest, and check your own call log to see when that is. Your plain-answer pages and Google profile keep working all year and cost nothing but time.
