Advertising

Referrals look you up, then call another lawyer? Law firm marketing that earns the call, honestly.

Someone gave a potential client your name. They looked you up, then called someone else. Lawyers also have bar rules about what they can say in ads. My take: the honest version that follows those rules is also the version people trust. Here's how a small firm can earn that call.

The ethics rules come first

Your state bar sets the rules for attorney advertising. In California, Rule 7.1 says a lawyer “shall not make a false or misleading communication” about their services, and the comments say an express guarantee of a result counts as misleading (State Bar of California). Rules on testimonials, specialist titles and disclaimers differ by state, so check yours before any campaign. My take: what the rules ban, like hype and guarantees, is also what makes people distrust lawyers.

Answer the questions people search first

My take: many people search their legal problem before they search for a lawyer. Think “what happens after a DUI in California”, “how long does probate take”, or “do I need a lawyer for this”. Write plain answers to those questions on your site. Skip the jargon, and be honest when someone doesn't need a lawyer. That builds trust before they ever call. Google says there's no special trick needed to show up in its AI features, and that it's worth making sure your important content is in text form (Google Search Central).

Make referrals a habit

My take: a lot of small-firm work comes from referrals. That means past clients, other attorneys, and people like CPAs, realtors or therapists, depending on your practice area. Make it a habit. Keep your profile complete and your site clear, because many referred clients look you up before they call. Stay in touch with the people who send you work. Where your state's rules allow it, ask happy clients for reviews in their own words. The referral gets you the name. What they find online helps decide the call.

Questions people actually ask

How do small law firms get clients?

My take: mostly through referrals that get checked online. Someone recommends you, and the person looks you up before calling. Plain answers on your site, a complete profile, and reviews where your bar rules allow them help that check go your way. The same basics help when someone searches “[practice area] attorney [city]”.

Can lawyers use client testimonials in marketing?

It depends on your state bar. Rules on testimonials and disclaimers differ from state to state. Check yours before you collect or post any. Where they're limited, plain answers to common legal questions can build the trust instead.

Is SEO worth it for a small law firm?

My take: it can be, but ask hard questions first. Start with the basics: a complete profile, plain answers to real client questions, and the same name, address and phone everywhere. Then ask any SEO firm which exact searches they're going after and how they'll show you results. Be wary of anyone who promises rankings.

How do lawyers show up in AI recommendations?

My take: make it easy for them to read and check you. Have a clear page for each practice area, your real credentials, the same info everywhere, and plain answers to common questions. Nobody can promise an AI tool will recommend you. Clear, plain pages give it something real to go on.
