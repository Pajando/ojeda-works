Law firm marketing: the honest playbook.

Legal marketing has a constraint no other industry does: bar rules govern what you can say. The honest playbook starts there — and it turns out the compliant version is also the version that wins trust. Here's how small firms actually get clients.

The ethics rules come first

Attorney advertising is regulated by your state bar: no misleading claims, no guaranteed outcomes, testimonial and specialization rules that vary by state, required disclaimers in some formats. Before any campaign, know your state's rules — and notice the alignment: everything the rules prohibit (hype, guarantees, inflated claims) is also what makes potential clients distrust lawyers. Compliant marketing and credible marketing are the same marketing.

Authority content is the engine

People search their legal problems long before they search for lawyers: “what happens after a DUI in California”, “how long does probate take”, “do I need a lawyer for X”. Plain-word answers to those questions — jargon-free, honest about when someone doesn't need a lawyer — build the authority that converts when the searcher becomes a client. It's also precisely what search engines and AI assistants read when someone asks them for a lawyer recommendation.

Referrals, systematized

Most small-firm business is referred — from past clients, other attorneys, and adjacent professionals (CPAs, realtors, therapists depending on practice area). Systematize it: stay findable and impressive when the referral checks you out online (they always do), keep the referring network warm, and ask satisfied clients for reviews where your state's rules permit, in their own words. The referral closes on your online presence — the handoff is only the beginning.

Questions people actually ask

How do small law firms get clients?

Referrals verified online: someone recommends you, and the prospect checks your presence before calling. Authority content, complete profiles, and permitted reviews make that check a conversion instead of a leak. Direct search (“[practice area] attorney [city]”) rewards the same foundation.

Can lawyers use client testimonials in marketing?

It depends on your state bar — some permit with disclaimers, some restrict heavily. Check your rules before collecting or displaying any. Where restricted, authority content carries the trust load testimonials would.

Is SEO worth it for a small law firm?

Legal SEO retainers run famously expensive because legal clicks are gold. Before a retainer: the foundation (profile, authority content in plain words, consistent info) captures much of the value, and demands evidence-based targeting — which specific searched questions will you own? Vague ‘rankings’ promises deserve the skepticism lawyers apply everywhere else.

How do lawyers show up in AI recommendations?

AI assistants recommend attorneys they can read and verify: clear practice-area pages, real credentials, consistent information, plain-language authority content. Most firm sites are unreadable legalese to those systems — the plain-words firm wins the new referral channel.
