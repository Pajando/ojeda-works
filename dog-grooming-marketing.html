Content & Social

Empty slots on the book? Dog grooming marketing that fills them honestly.

Open slots on the book and regulars who drift away cost you money every week. My take: dog owners rarely leave a groomer they trust, so the job is simple. Look trustworthy to the nervous first-timer, and never let a client leave without the next appointment.

Before-and-after photos do the selling

A matted doodle that leaves looking like a teddy bear is easy content to post. My take: before-and-afters are a groomer's best free ad, so make them a habit. Shoot every big change (with the owner's OK, always), name the dog, tag the town. Post the calm handling of nervous dogs too. Owners of nervous dogs are looking for exactly that.

Empty slots fill at pickup

Coats grow back, so every pickup is a chance to fill a future slot. Book the next visit at pickup, every time, and send a reminder when it's due. My take: a groomer whose regulars always rebook needs far less new-client marketing. Offer standing appointments to regulars and keep a waitlist for the rest. A real waitlist is honest proof you're good.

The foundation and what to skip

Start with a complete Google Business Profile: hours, services, and lots of current photos of real dogs you groomed. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results. Ask every client for an honest review, not just the happy ones, and never tell them what to write. Google's review rules ban asking only happy customers. Add a simple site with your services, prices by size and coat, and how you handle nervous or senior dogs, in plain text, so owners with those worries find the answer. What to skip, my take: discounts to fill gaps (they bring one-time bargain hunters) and overbooking to chase revenue. One rushed groom that nicks a dog costs more than a slow Tuesday ever will.

Questions people actually ask

How do dog groomers get new clients?

Before-and-after photos and honest reviews from all your clients. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results, so keep your profile full and your photos current. Referrals from vets and daycares help too.

How do groomers keep clients coming back?

Book the next appointment at pickup, remind when the cycle is due, and offer standing slots to regulars. Coats grow back, so the work repeats. My take: groomers who make rebooking a habit spend less time chasing new clients.

What should a dog groomer post on social media?

Transformations, nervous-dog wins, breed-specific cuts, the occasional behind-the-scenes bath chaos. Name the dogs, tag the town, always get permission. My take: three real posts a week beat daily filler.

Should a dog groomer raise prices or take more dogs?

My take: usually prices. A full book with a waitlist means you can charge more; more volume per day risks the rushed grooms that damage the trust the whole business runs on. Raise gently, grandfather regulars for a cycle, and communicate early.
