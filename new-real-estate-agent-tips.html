No listings and nobody calling yet? New real estate agent tips for an honest first year.

You got your license. Now the phone is quiet and nobody hands you a listing. My take: in year one you have to give people reasons to pick you before you have closings to show. Here's how to do that without the guru talk.

Your first clients already know you

My take: your first deals will most likely come from people who already know you. Friends, family, old coworkers, the gym, your league. Tell all of them, clearly and without feeling weird about it: what you do, which towns you work, and that you grow by referrals. Then give them something to repeat. Call back fast and explain things in plain words. People talk about the agent who did that, and in year one that talk is most of your marketing.

Build proof before you have closings

No sold homes yet? You can still show you know the area. Pick one part of town (agents call it a farm area), learn it well, and share what you learn: market updates in plain words, what's changing on the main streets, the questions buyers ask you. Talk about the homes and the places, not about who lives there, and ask your broker to look over your posts and ads before they go out. Set up the basics on day one: complete profiles, a simple site people can read, and the same name, phone and email everywhere. That keeps working for you while you wait for your first closing.

What to skip in year one

Skip three things. First, buying lots of leads before you know what a closing from them costs you. If a lead is shared, you're racing other agents for it on a thin budget. Second, big brand ads before anyone knows your name. Third, any shortcut that fakes it: made-up reviews, padded numbers, sales that aren't yours. My take: people are trusting you with one of the biggest purchases of their life. A short, honest record beats a big, fake one.

Questions people actually ask

How do new real estate agents get their first clients?

My take: from people who already trust them. Tell your friends, family and old coworkers exactly what you do and where. Then give fast, honest service so they have a good story to pass on. Offering to host open houses for busier agents in your office also puts you in front of buyers who don't have an agent yet. Ask your broker first.

Should new agents buy leads?

My take: not first. Bought leads can eat a small budget fast, and a shared lead means other agents are calling the same person. Put that money into your profile, your farm area and posts that show local knowledge, because those keep working. If you do test leads, track what each closing cost you.

How long until a new agent makes money?

My take: plan for months, not weeks. A deal can take a long time from first chat to closing, and agents are usually paid when it closes. Save enough to get through a lean first year. Referrals from year one are what can make year two easier.

What should a new agent post on social media?

What you're learning and what you know: market updates in plain words, local spots worth knowing, and the honest story of your first year. You don't need closings to be useful. My take: posts that show you know the area give people, and search tools, something to find when they start looking for an agent.
