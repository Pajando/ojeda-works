Paying for SEO and the phone still isn't ringing? Here's how much SEO should cost a small business.

My take: SEO retainers often run from about $500 to $5,000 or more a month. That's my estimate, not a survey. A lot of what a small business needs isn't a monthly retainer at all. And there's a newer question to ask anyone selling SEO: do they check whether AI tools recommend you?

What SEO retainers commonly cost

My take: monthly SEO often runs $500 to $5,000 or more, depending on your market and how much work gets done. Some of that buys real work: content, technical fixes, local listings. Some buys reports about rankings for words nobody searches. Ask any SEO one question: “which searches will I show up for, and how do you know people type them?” If they can't show you, keep your money. And walk away from anyone who promises the top spot. Google says it plainly: “No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google” (Google Search Central).

The one-time work that does most of the lifting

My take: for a local business, most of the work that pays off is done once. A complete Google Business Profile, a site built around what people really search, fast pages with schema markup, the same info on every listing, and honest reviews. Google says it plainly: “Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results” (Google Business Profile Help). That's a project, not a forever retainer. Monthly SEO earns its fee when there's new content worth making, not as a subscription to vague ‘optimization.’

The question many SEO packages skip

Some customers now ask ChatGPT or Google's AI who to hire. My take: those tools lean toward businesses they can read and check, and many SEO packages never look at this, so ask yours if they do. Answer Optimization Audit checks how easy your business is for AI tools to read and shows what's in the way. Classic SEO and AI visibility overlap, but they aren't the same thing.

Questions people actually ask

Is monthly SEO worth it for a small business?

It's worth it when it pays for real ongoing work, like new content for searches people really make, or a tough local market. It's not worth it as a subscription to reports. My take: many small businesses do better paying once to get the basics right, then spending again only when there's real work to do.

Why do SEO prices vary so much?

Because “SEO” covers everything from a profile cleanup to a content fight in a crowded market. Price follows how tough your market is and how much real work happens each month. My take: a $500 retainer in a small town can be honest, and a $5,000 one can be for show. Judge the work list, not the number.

How long does SEO take to work?

Nobody can honestly promise a date, and Google's own guide says “No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google” (Google Search Central). My take: fixes to your profile, indexing and site structure can show movement sooner, while content and reviews build over months. Ask anyone selling SEO what results they expect and by when, then hold them to it.

What's the difference between SEO and AI visibility?

SEO is about showing up in a list of links. AI visibility is about being the business named when someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI directly. They share the same base: sites that are easy to read, real reviews, and the same info everywhere. My take: AI tools add their own checks on top, and that's the part Answer Optimization Audit looks at.
