Content & Social

Why is my Instagram not growing?

First, the reframe that saves you months: for a local business, follower count is a vanity metric — a thousand followers in your town beat fifty thousand strangers who will never buy a haircut in Napa. But if the account is genuinely stuck, the causes are usually the same three, and Instagram has publicly explained enough of its ranking to diagnose them.

What Instagram says actually gets distributed

Instagram has publicly explained that there's no single algorithm — each surface (Feed, Reels, Explore) ranks on its own signals, built around how likely each viewer is to interact. In practice, and in Instagram's own later explanations, the signals that carry reach to strangers are watch time, likes, and especially sends — someone forwarding your post to a friend is the platform's strongest hint of value. Original content is favored over reposts. The translation for a business account: reach comes from posts people finish and forward, not from posting daily, not from hashtag stacking, and not from content the account's own followers scroll past — that last signal is precisely what stops distribution to anyone new.

The three usual causes, honestly

One: the content is about you, not for anyone — announcements, logos, “we're hiring,” re-posted flyers. Nobody sends a flyer to a friend. Two: it's indistinguishable — the same stock-quote graphics and holiday posts every competitor runs, which the feed correctly treats as furniture. Three: there's no reason a stranger would care — content aimed at existing customers only ever reaches existing customers. The fix is one editorial question before every post: would a person in your town send this to another person? Transformations, process, prices explained honestly, the weird job of the week — those get sent. Announcements get scrolled.

And the question behind the question

Ask what growing is for. If the goal is customers, check whether Instagram is even where your customers decide — for most local services, the deciding moments happen on Google search, the review comparison, and the website, none of which the feed touches. The honest allocation for most local businesses: a portfolio-quality post when there's real work to show, and the reclaimed hours into the machine-readable foundation that catches people at the buying moment. Growth on the platform is a means; if it stops serving the actual goal, stop feeding it.

Questions people actually ask

Why did my Instagram reach suddenly drop?

Usually a content pattern the feed stopped rewarding, not a penalty: reach falls when your own followers stop engaging, which cuts distribution to strangers too. Occasionally it's mechanical — reposted content with watermarks is deprioritized per Instagram's own guidance. Diagnose by what your last ten posts gave a stranger.

Do hashtags still grow an Instagram account?

Marginally at best — Instagram's own explanations center watch time, likes, and sends, not tags. A handful of specific local tags cost nothing, but no tag rescues a post nobody would forward. The content is the lever.

How often should a business post on Instagram?

When there's something real: for most local businesses two to four honest posts a week beats daily filler — and the feed's ranking agrees, since posts your own followers skip suppress your reach to strangers. Quality of the send-worthy kind is the frequency.

Should a small business buy followers?

Never — bought followers are strangers (or bots) who never engage, which teaches the ranking system your content isn't worth distributing, on top of being fake. A thousand real locals beat any purchased number, and they're the only ones who ever become customers.
