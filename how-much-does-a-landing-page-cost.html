Ad clicks but no calls? Landing page cost, and whether you even need one.

People click your ad, land on your page, and leave without calling. A landing page can start free with a builder or cost more if someone builds it for you. My take: the better question is whether you need one at all, or just a better page on the site you already have. Both answers below.

The price tiers

Builders: Carrd lets you start with a free account, and its paid plans are billed by the year (Carrd pricing). Others, like Unbounce, charge monthly (Unbounce pricing). Check the current price on their page, because it changes. My take: builders are fast and template-based, but you're renting. Ask what happens to your page if you stop paying.

A freelancer or small shop: my take is to expect a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars for one page built around one offer, with real copy and tracking set up. Get it in writing that you own the page and the files.

The monthly-fee trap: some vendors charge a monthly fee for a page that never changes. My take: if nobody is testing and improving the page, it doesn't need a subscription. Ask what the monthly fee actually pays for.

What makes a landing page earn its cost

One offer, one audience, one action. The page should keep the promise the ad made, in the same words, with no menu pulling people away. Google Ads rates this as “landing page experience”: how relevant and useful your page is to people who click your ad (Google Ads Help). My take: the words matter more than the design. A plain page that answers the visitor's exact question beats a pretty one that talks about your company.

When you don't need one

My take: if you're not running ads or a specific campaign, you probably need better site pages, not a separate landing page. A service page built around what customers actually search for, with clear answers and a plain next step, does the same job. And it can keep bringing in visitors from search after the campaign ends.

Quick answers

What's the difference between a landing page and a website page?

A landing page serves one campaign: one offer, one action, often with no menu. A website page is part of your whole site and is there for people who find you through search. Ads point at landing pages. Search engines and AI tools mostly find site pages. My take: most small businesses should fix their site pages first.

Why do landing pages have monthly fees?

Because the builder hosts the page and rents you the tools. That's fair while you're actively testing versions. My take: it's just rent when the page sits there unchanged. A simple page you own can be hosted cheaply, so move it once the campaign becomes permanent.

How many landing pages do I need for ads?

One per offer or audience, not one per ad. If every ad says the same thing to the same people, one well-built page is enough. My take: split only when the promise really changes.

Do landing pages help with Google rankings?

Don't count on it. A landing page's job is turning paid clicks into calls, and some are hidden from search on purpose. My take: for search, build real site pages that answer real questions. The two jobs need different pages.
