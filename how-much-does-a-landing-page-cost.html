Website Development

How much does a landing page cost?

Commonly free to a few thousand dollars — but the more useful question is whether you need a landing page or just a better page on the site you already have. Both answers below, honestly.

The price tiers

Builders (Unbounce, Leadpages, Carrd tiers): commonly $0–$100+ a month. Fast, template-based, and rented — stop paying and the page vanishes.

A freelancer or small shop: commonly a few hundred to a couple thousand for a page built around one offer, with real copy and tracking wired in. You own it.

The monthly-fee trap: some vendors charge ongoing fees for a static page that never changes. A landing page that isn't being tested and improved doesn't need a subscription — ask what the monthly fee actually does.

What makes a landing page earn its cost

One offer, one audience, one action. The page exists to close the loop an ad or link opened — same promise, same language, no navigation leaks. The copy matters far more than the design: a plain page that answers the visitor's exact question outperforms a beautiful one that talks about your company.

When you don't need one

If you're not running ads or a specific campaign, you likely need better site pages, not a separate landing page. A service page built around what customers actually search — with clear answers and a plain next step — does the same job and keeps earning organic traffic after the campaign ends. Landing pages rent attention; site pages own it.

Questions people actually ask

What's the difference between a landing page and a website page?

A landing page serves one campaign: one offer, one action, usually no navigation. A website page serves ongoing search traffic and the whole site's structure. Ads point at landing pages; Google and AI recommend site pages. Most small businesses need great site pages first.

Why do landing pages have monthly fees?

Because builders host them and rent you the tools. That's legitimate while you're actively testing variants; it's rent when the page just sits there. A static page you own can be hosted for pennies — worth it the moment a campaign becomes permanent.

How many landing pages do I need for ads?

One per distinct offer or audience — not one per ad. If every ad says the same thing to the same people, one well-built page beats five thin ones. Split only when the promise genuinely differs.

Do landing pages help with Google rankings?

Usually not — most are set to hide from search or too thin to rank. That's fine; their job is converting paid traffic. For rankings, build real site pages that answer real queries. The two jobs need different pages.
