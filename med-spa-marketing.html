Content & Social

Lots of likes, not enough consults? Med spa marketing that earns trust, honestly.

Your posts get likes. The consult calendar stays thin. My take: people want the result, but first they need to trust the person holding the needle. A lot of med spa marketing shows off the result and hides the person. Here's how to flip that.

Credentials are the content

Who does the injecting? What are they licensed for? Who is the medical director? How many of this treatment have they done? Put the answers on your site in plain text, with real names and faces. My take: the person who has watched your page for weeks is waiting for those safety answers. For before-and-afters, use only your own patients, with their written OK, the same lighting each time, no filters, and an honest label of what was done and when. Ask your medical director what your state lets you show and say.

Memberships and rebooking are the engine

Many treatments wear off or come in a series. That's repeat business if you follow up every time: ask them to rebook at checkout, send a reminder when it's time, and offer a membership that makes the routine easy. My take: chasing new faces with discounts while your regulars drift away is doing it backwards.

The foundation and what to skip

Fill out your Google Business Profile completely, with services and starting prices. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results (Google Business Profile Help). Ask happy clients to mention their provider by name, in their own words. Put licenses, the products you use, and how a consult works on your site as text. Google's guide to its AI search features says they need no special optimization, and it lists putting important content in text form among the basics (Google Search Central). What to skip: deep-discount deals that can fill your book with one-time visitors, and ever using someone else's before-and-after photos. It's dishonest, and it breaks the trust this whole page is about.

Questions people actually ask

How do med spas get new clients?

Build trust in public: named providers with their credentials, honest before-and-afters of your own patients, prices on the site, and reviews that mention real people. My take: Instagram gets you noticed, and clear safety answers on your site get the consult booked.

Should a med spa list prices online?

My take: yes. Hidden prices can feel expensive and salesy to a nervous first-timer. List starting prices or ranges, with a note that every plan is set at the consult. Being open about price sets you apart.

Do med spas need a medical director for marketing claims?

Ask your medical director and your state medical board, not a marketing person. Rules on what you can claim, which titles you can use, and who can do which treatment can differ by state and license. Once it's right, say it plainly, like who supervises and who injects. My take: that answers the safety question people are afraid to ask out loud.

What social media works best for med spas?

My take: Instagram and TikTok, used to teach more than to show off. Explain what a treatment actually does, show what recovery really looks like, and post meet-the-injector videos. Teaching builds trust. Constant promos wear it down.
