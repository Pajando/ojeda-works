Med spa marketing: the honest playbook.

A med spa sells two things at once: a result and the confidence that the person holding the needle knows what they're doing. Most med spa marketing screams the first and whispers the second — which is exactly backwards for how clients actually choose.

Credentials are the content

Who does the injecting, what are they licensed in, who's the medical director, how many of this procedure have they done — put it on the site in plain text, with real faces and names. Prospects lurk for weeks before booking; the practice that answers the safety questions openly wins the lurkers. Before-and-afters with written consent, shot in consistent lighting, labeled honestly with units and timing, beat any filtered promo.

Memberships and rebooking are the engine

Botox wears off on a schedule; skin treatments run in series. That's recurring revenue if the follow-up is systematic — a rebooking ask at checkout, a reminder timed to the product's actual duration, a membership that makes the routine automatic. A med spa with 300 members doesn't sweat slow weeks. Chasing new faces with discounts while regulars slip away is running the machine in reverse.

The foundation and what to skip

Complete profile with services and prices — hiding prices creates fear, and fear kills bookings in this industry. Reviews that mention the specific provider by name. A site that states license, products used, and consult process in text machines can read, because “best med spa near me” is now a question people ask AI assistants. What to skip: Groupon-style deep discounts that fill the book with one-timers and train clients to wait for sales, and borrowing before-and-after photos — that's fraud, and clients reverse-image-search.

Questions people actually ask

How do med spas get new clients?

Trust built in public: named providers with credentials, consistent honest before-and-afters, prices on the site, and reviews that mention specific people. Instagram brings the lurkers; the website's safety answers convert them.

Should a med spa list prices online?

Yes. Hidden prices read as “expensive and salesy” and stop nervous first-timers from booking a consult. List starting prices or ranges with a note that plans are individual — transparency is a competitive weapon in this industry.

Do med spas need a medical director for marketing claims?

Marketing must match what your state allows for your license — claims, titles, and who performs what. Get the compliance right first; it's also a selling point. “Physician-supervised” in plain text answers the fear question prospects won't say out loud.

What social media works best for med spas?

Instagram and TikTok, used for education over glamour: what a unit of Botox actually does, what recovery looks like, meet-the-injector videos. Educational content builds the trust that promotional content burns.
