Video

Got a video quote that made your stomach drop? Commercial video production cost, explained honestly.

You asked for one short video and got a quote that made no sense. Or three quotes that don't match. My take: the word commercial hides the real question. What does the video have to do, and where will it run? Here's what drives the price.

The tiers

Solo videographer: My take: often a few thousand dollars for a business commercial that is written, shot, and edited. This is the tier most small businesses look at first.

Small production company: My take: often five figures. This adds crew, better gear, locations, and casting. It can make sense when the spot runs as a paid ad and the polish has to pay for itself.

Full production: My take: this can reach six figures, with TV campaigns and known actors. Most local businesses don't need it. These ranges are my estimates, not survey data, so get two or three written quotes.

What actually drives the budget

Shoot days, crew size, locations, talent, and revision rounds. My take: the hidden driver is the planning before the shoot. A tight script and shot plan makes a one-day shoot deliver what a sloppy three-day one can't. If a quote is high, ask which of these it's paying for. A good producer should be able to break it down line by line.

The AI-assisted middle path

Some spots don't need your real shop or real people on camera: an offer announcement, an explainer, a concept ad. My take: for those, AI-assisted video can cost much less than a shoot. It's the wrong tool when the point is to show your real place and your real face. Ask any producer to price both ways and tell you which one fits.

Questions people actually ask

How much does a 30-second commercial cost?

My take: from a solo shooter, often a few thousand dollars all-in; from a production company, often five figures. Length matters less than you'd think. Planning, shoot days, and editing drive the cost, so a tight 30 seconds can cost about the same as a loose 60.

Where should a small business run a commercial?

Where your customers already are. My take: start with social ads, your own site, and your Google profile, because you can measure them and test them cheaply. Broadcast TV last, if ever; it reaches far more people than most local businesses need.

Is AI-generated video professional enough for a commercial?

My take: for simple concept spots, it can be. For anything that must show your real business or your real face, no. Judge each project by one question: what has to be real, and what only has to be clear?

What should I have ready before getting a quote?

The one thing the video must accomplish, where it will run, and any hard constraints (budget range, deadline, must-show elements). With those three, an honest producer can quote realistically; without them, expect quotes padded for the unknown.
