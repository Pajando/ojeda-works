Website Development

Customers can't check you out before they call? Here's when a small business needs a website.

Before people call, they look you up on their phone. If all they find is a map pin and a few reviews, it's easy to call someone else. You're not alone: we checked 257 local businesses in Napa and Solano counties and 52 had no website at all, about 1 in 5. My take: if new customers ever search for what you do, yes, you need one. Not to look fancy. So there's something of yours to read when they look.

The real reason, in plain terms

My take: when someone asks Google or ChatGPT “who's the best [what you do] near me,” those systems build their answer from what they can read: websites, business profiles, reviews, directories. With no website, all they have is your listing and what other people wrote about you. Your prices, your services and why you're good stay in your head, not online. So when a competitor's page answers those questions and yours doesn't exist, it's easier for those systems to send the customer to them.

A Facebook or Instagram page helps, but it isn't the same thing. You don't own it. The platform sets the rules, decides who sees your posts, and can shut the account down. Your website is the one piece you control.

When you genuinely don't need one yet

Honest exceptions exist. If every customer comes by referral and you're fully booked, a website probably won't change much. If the business is a short-term experiment, a complete Google Business Profile might carry it. The real question: do new customers ever search for what you do? If they do and can't find you, you need one.

What a small business site actually needs

Less than you're usually sold. One page that says clearly what you do, where, and for whom. Your real contact information matching your Google Business Profile exactly. Answers to the questions customers actually ask. Fast loading on a phone. A short block of code called schema markup that tells machines your name, hours and phone; Google says it uses this to understand a page. That's it. My take: five clear pages beat twenty-five confusing ones.

Questions people actually ask

Can I just use a Facebook page instead of a website?

You can, but you're renting, not owning. The platform decides who sees your posts, and if the account gets suspended, your whole presence goes with it. My take: a simple website of your own plus a social page beats a social page alone.

Is a Google Business Profile enough without a website?

It's free, and for some businesses it carries a lot. Google's own help page says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence is also based on “how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have.” A website gives Google and AI apps more of your own words to read. My take: start with the profile if you have to choose, and plan for both.

How many pages does a small business website need?

Usually fewer than you're sold. One clear page for each real thing you do, plus contact and about. My take: that's often five to ten pages. What matters is that each page answers a question real customers search for, not the page count.

What makes a website readable to AI?

Plain words that say what you do and where, a clean layout, and schema markup: a short block of code that tells machines your name, address, hours and phone. Google says it uses this kind of code to understand a page. Prices on the page help too, so a machine can answer “how much?” My take: a page that loads fast on a phone helps as well.
