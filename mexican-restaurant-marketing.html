Empty tables on a Tuesday? Mexican restaurant marketing that earns the visit, honestly.

The food is great, and the dining room is still half empty on a Tuesday. My take: you have two things the chains can't copy. Food that looks amazing on a phone, and real roots in a community that speaks Spanish and English. Use both. Here's how.

The food does the talking

Steam off the comal, the cheese pull, the salsa going on, the plate hitting the table. Shoot short phone clips during real service. Post them before lunch and before dinner, when people are deciding where to eat, and name the dish and the town. Fill your Google profile with real dish photos too. My take: when someone searches “tacos near me,” your photos do the selling before they ever call.

Post in both languages, for real

Post in Spanish AND English. Write each one for real, not as a stiff translation of the other. The family deciding where to eat Sunday lunch may search in either language. Do the same on your site: the menu as real text in both languages, not a photo of the menu. Google's guide to its AI search features says to put important content in text form (Google Search Central).

Community roots as strategy

The regulars, the quinceañera you catered, the local team you fed, the story behind the family recipe. A chain can't fake that. Share it honestly, with permission from the people in the photos. Give catering its own page (“catering [town]”) with real event photos and a quick way to ask for a quote. My take: one big party order can be worth a lot of regular tickets, and the person planning it is often looking online.

Questions people actually ask

How do I get more customers to my Mexican restaurant?

Show up when people are hungry and searching: a Google profile full of real dish photos and reviews, your menu as text on a fast site, and food clips posted before mealtimes, in both languages. My take: posting in Spanish too can reach people your English posts miss.

Should I post in Spanish or English?

My take: both, and write each one for real instead of translating word for word. A lot of your customers live in both languages, and some search in Spanish first. Posting in Spanish shows them the place is for them too.

What's the best social platform for a restaurant?

Wherever your town actually scrolls. Often that's Instagram and TikTok for food clips and Facebook for community news and events. Post the same clip on all of them. My take: posting real food before mealtimes matters more than which app you pick.

How do I market catering for my restaurant?

Give catering its own page with real event photos, clear packages, and a quick way to ask for a quote. After every event, ask for a review. My take: the person planning a party compares options online, so a clear catering page with proof can bring in orders that walk-in traffic never would.
