Video

Paid for a video and the phone stayed quiet? A promo video for your business, built around one true point.

You paid for the video. It looks nice. Nobody called. If nobody decided what the video had to prove, that's where it went wrong, before the camera ever turned on. Here's how to plan one, what to ask before you pay, and what to skip.

Decide the one point first

My take: a promo video that tries to say everything ends up saying nothing. Pick one thing: you're fast, you're careful, you're local, you've done this for years. Pick the one your best customers would say about you. Then build every shot around showing it. Don't say it. Show it.

The test: if your video's script could run over a competitor's footage without anyone noticing, it isn't about your business yet.

What to ask before you pay

Prices swing a lot depending on who shoots it, how many days it takes, and how much editing it needs. I won't quote a market number I can't back up, so get two or three quotes in writing and ask each one the same things. What one point does this video prove? How long is it, and do I get a vertical version for phones and a wide one for my site? Who owns the footage? AI tools can also make explainer-style videos without a shoot. My take: that's fine when nothing real has to be shown, and wrong when customers need to see your real face, your real place or your real work.

Where it earns its keep

Put it where people deciding about you already look: your homepage, your Google Business Profile, and the top of your social profiles. Google's Business Profile video guidelines say videos can be up to 30 seconds long, so cut a short version for your profile (Google Business Profile Help). I'd keep a promo video where new customers pass by, not let it scroll away in a feed after a day.

Questions people actually ask

What should a small business promo video include?

One true claim proven on camera, a real look at the work or place, one human face customers will actually meet, and a plain next step at the end. What it should skip: mission-statement voiceovers, stock footage, and every adjective that could describe any business.

How much should a small business spend on a promo video?

Prices vary a lot by who shoots it, how many days it takes and how much editing it needs. Get two or three written quotes. I'd start with one good video that proves one point, see if it brings calls, and only then think about spending big.

Is an AI-generated promo video good enough?

It depends on the job. For an explainer where nothing real has to be shown, it can work and can cost less than a shoot. When customers need to see your real place, your real work or your real people, I'd say no. Faking that can cost you their trust.

How long should a business promo video be?

My take: as long as it takes to prove your one point, and no longer. Cut every second that doesn't help prove it. If it takes you three minutes to make your point, the problem is the point, not the length.
