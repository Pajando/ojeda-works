Home services marketing: the playbook every trade shares.

Plumber, electrician, HVAC, roofer, cleaner — different trades, same four marketing physics: trust, urgency, reviews, recurring revenue. Master the shared playbook, then apply your trade's twist. Both below.

The four physics of home services

Trust: you enter homes — license, insurance, real photos, and human professionalism convert before price. Urgency: much of the demand searches at the crisis moment and picks the first credible answer. Reviews: the neighbor's word, scaled — asked at the moment of relief, in the customer's own words, naming job and town. Recurring: plans and maintenance turn crisis customers into relationships that never re-shop. Every effective tactic in every trade serves one of these four.

The shared foundation

Complete Google Business Profile with honest availability, a fast site with a page per service in real text, consistent info everywhere, and steady review velocity. This is what search engines and AI assistants read when someone asks who to call — and in most towns, most competitors have it half-built. Finishing it is the whole edge.

Your trade's twist

Each trade bends the physics its own way — the per-trade playbooks: plumbers (urgency above all), electricians (the emergency/project split), HVAC (the seasonal calendar), roofers (the anti-storm-chaser play), pest control (discretion plus recurrence), cleaning (trust plus recurring), contractors (proof against fear), and landscaping (the visible-work advantage).

Questions people actually ask

What marketing works best for home service businesses?

The foundation beats every tactic: complete profile, service pages machines can read, review velocity, and answered phones. Paid ads amplify it; nothing replaces it. The four physics — trust, urgency, reviews, recurrence — decide every channel's ROI.

Should home service companies hire a marketing agency?

Read the deliverables first: much of what home-service agencies retainer-ize is the one-time foundation above. Pay for genuine ongoing work (content, active ad management with reported cost-per-job) — not a subscription to reports. One person who knows the trade often beats a generic agency.

How important are reviews for home services?

They're the deciding factor — urgent searchers pick from what they read in ninety seconds, and AI assistants weigh the same signals. Velocity and specificity beat raw totals: recent reviews naming the job and town outsell a big stale number.

How do home service companies show up in AI recommendations?

AI assistants recommend what they can read and verify: complete consistent profiles, real reviews, service pages in plain text. It's the same foundation, read by a new judge — and checking how you score with that judge is what AO Audit was built for.
