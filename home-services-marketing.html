Advertising

Phone not ringing? The marketing basics every home service trade shares.

When the phone goes quiet, it's easy to blame the season. My take: more often, the people with a leak, a dead AC or a bad roof just aren't finding you, trusting you or reaching you fast enough. Plumber, electrician, HVAC, roofer, cleaner: different trades, same four things to get right. Get the shared basics first, then add your trade's twist. Both are below.

Four things every trade has to get right

My take on the four: Trust: you go into people's homes, so show your license, your insurance, and real photos of your crew and your work. Speed: a lot of calls come in the middle of a mess, and the first business that looks solid and picks up often gets the job. Reviews: they're the neighbor's word, for strangers. Ask right after you fix the problem, and let customers say it in their own words, naming the job and the town. That part isn't just me. Google says "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." Repeat customers: service plans and tune-ups turn a one-time emergency into a customer who calls you first next time.

The shared foundation

Fill out your Google Business Profile all the way, with hours you actually keep. Build a fast website with one page for each service, written in real text. Keep your name, address, and phone number the same everywhere. Keep new reviews coming in. Why it matters, in Google's own words: "If your business info isn't accurate, your Business Profile might not show up for relevant searches in your area." And for Google's AI answers: "The best practices for SEO remain relevant for AI features in Google Search (such as AI Overviews and AI Mode)." My take: a lot of local businesses leave this half done, so finishing it is one of the cheapest edges you can get.

Your trade's twist

Each trade has its own twist. Here's the guide for each one: plumbers (urgency above all), electricians (the emergency/project split), HVAC (the seasonal calendar), roofers (the anti-storm-chaser play), pest control (discretion plus recurrence), cleaning (trust plus recurring), contractors (proof against fear), landscaping (the visible-work advantage), painters (proof of prep), movers (the anti-horror-story play), junk removal (the urgent search), handymen (reliability made visible), tree services (credentials up front), and pool service (route density).

Questions people actually ask

What marketing works best for home service businesses?

My take: get the basics done before you pay for anything else. That means a complete Google profile, a page for each service in plain text, steady new reviews, and a phone someone answers. Ads can bring more calls on top of that, but they don't fix a weak profile.

Should home service companies hire a marketing agency?

My take: read the list of what you're paying for first. A lot of what gets sold as a monthly plan is setup work you only need once. Pay monthly for real ongoing work, like new content, or ads where they tell you what each job cost you. Don't pay monthly just for reports.

How important are reviews for home services?

Google's guide says more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking. My take: recent reviews that name the job and the town help more than a big pile of old ones, because they show a homeowner you did this exact work nearby, lately.

How do home service companies show up in AI recommendations?

For Google's AI answers, Google says the usual best practices still apply and there are no extra requirements. My take: that means the same basics. A complete profile that matches everywhere, real reviews, and service pages in plain text. Other AI assistants don't spell out how they pick, so nobody can honestly promise you a spot.
