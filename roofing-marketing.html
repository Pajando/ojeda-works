Homeowners calling the other roofer? Roofing marketing that earns the call, honestly.

Homeowners have heard about storm chasers: the knock on the door, then the contractor who disappears. My take: the honest local roofer wins by being the clear opposite. Here's how.

Be the anti-storm-chaser

My take: trust signals matter a lot in this trade. Show your local address, your license number and insurance, years in the area, and warranty terms in writing. Ask for reviews at the final walkthrough, in the homeowner's own words, with the town named. When your online presence says “we were here before the storm and we'll be here after,” you look like the opposite of the door-knocker.

Proof from above

Roofing has a visibility problem: customers can't see the work up there. Drone photos and video fix that. Before-and-after aerials show the work, document the job for the homeowner, and make strong content. Use a pilot with an FAA remote pilot certificate (FAA rule 14 CFR 107.12). My take: a drone shot of a finished roof, posted with the town, is the roofing ad that doesn't feel like one.

Storms, insurance, and steady work

When a storm hits, homeowners start looking for a roofer fast. My take: the roofers who win those weeks already had their profile, reviews, and pages ready. Talk honestly about insurance claims: help homeowners through the process, never promise an outcome, and never offer to cover or waive a deductible, which can be illegal depending on your state. Between storms, inspection offers, gutter and maintenance work, and repair content (“how long does a roof last”, “repair or replace”) keep work coming when the weather doesn't.

Questions people actually ask

How do roofers get customers without door knocking?

Be easy to find when the homeowner searches: a complete Google profile, drone-photo proof, real local reviews, and pages that answer the repair-or-replace questions. The knock interrupts; the search invites. My take: building for the search is what sets a local company apart from storm chasers.

Does drone footage really help roofing marketing?

My take: yes, because it shows work customers otherwise never see, documents the job, and makes strong content. Under the FAA's Part 107 rule, the person flying needs a remote pilot certificate or must fly under direct supervision of someone who has one. Hire a certified pilot or get certified yourself.

How should roofers handle insurance-claim marketing?

Honestly and carefully: help homeowners through the process, document damage well, and never promise approvals or offer to cover a deductible. Depending on your state, that can be illegal. My take: the honest version is also the one that wins trust.

What keeps roofing work steady between storms?

Inspections, maintenance, and repair services marketed year-round, plus content that catches early-stage searches (“roof leak repair [town]”, “how long does a roof last”). The storm business rides weather; the inspection business rides the calendar you control.
