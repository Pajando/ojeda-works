Video

Video marketing for small business: the honest playbook.

Here's the asymmetry nobody exploits: customers watch video more than anything else, and most small businesses produce exactly none. You don't need a channel, a schedule, or a studio. You need two or three honest videos doing specific jobs — and the bar, set by your competitors, is on the floor.

The two videos that matter first

Video one: the 60-90 second answer to “who are these people and can I trust them” — the owner, on camera, saying what you do, for whom, and why you do it that way. It lives on your homepage and your profile, and it converts the fence-sitters who almost called. Video two: the proof — a real job start to finish, a real transformation, a real customer (with permission) saying what happened in their own words. Neither needs to be polished; both need to be true. A phone, daylight, and a person who means what they're saying beat a drone intro and a stock-music montage every time.

Where the videos actually work

The same video does different jobs in different places: on the website it converts visitors who arrived from search; on the Google profile it makes you the only business in the comparison with a face; cut into 30-second verticals it becomes the social content you were struggling to invent; in a quote email (“here's who'd be doing the work”) it closes bids. That's the efficiency most businesses miss — you're not feeding a video channel, you're making one asset and spending it five ways.

What to skip

Skip the brand anthem — the cinematic logo-reveal piece that says nothing a customer asked. Skip scripting the soul out of it; owners reading teleprompters convert worse than owners talking. Skip daily-content ambitions that die in week three and leave a dead channel as public evidence. And skip waiting for confidence — the owner who's visibly nervous but real outperforms the one who never filmed anything, because the competitor benchmark is silence. When the job needs more than a phone — a story worth telling properly, a series, an ad — that's what our video division does.

Questions people actually ask

Is video marketing worth it for a small business?

Yes, disproportionately — because almost none of your local competitors do it. One honest owner-on-camera video and one proof-of-work video cover the highest-value jobs: trust at the moment of comparison, and evidence at the moment of decision.

How much does small business video marketing cost?

The first versions can cost nothing but nerve: a phone, daylight, and honesty. Professionally produced pieces for a small business commonly run from hundreds into a few thousand dollars depending on scope — worth it once the free versions prove where video pays for you.

What should a small business's first video be?

The owner, 60-90 seconds, answering who you are, what you do, and why you do it that way. Put it on the homepage and the Google profile. It's the video that converts the people already deciding — which makes it the highest-return minute you can film.

Do videos help a business show up on Google?

Indirectly but really: video keeps visitors on pages longer, makes profiles more complete, and gives AI assistants and searchers proof a business is active and real. The direct win is conversion — the person already looking picks the business with the face.
