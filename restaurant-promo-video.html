Video

Empty tables on a Tuesday? A restaurant promo video that brings diners in, not just views.

My take: the best restaurant videos don't look like ads. They look like proof: real food coming out of a real kitchen, real people eating it. Here's what to film, where to post it, and what to skip.

What to film instead of polish

The dish-build shot: one signature dish made start to finish, cut fast. The rush shot: motion, steam, and noise from the kitchen. The regular's order: a real customer (with permission), their usual, one sentence about why. And the owner saying one true thing about why the place exists, in thirty seconds, no script.

My take: skip drone shots of the building, fake-looking slow motion, and reading the menu out loud. People pick a restaurant on hunger and trust, so every second should feed one of the two.

What it commonly costs

My take: a short promo from a solo videographer can run from a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars, depending on shoot time and editing, so get quotes. Ask for one shoot cut into several short clips, because the same kitchen footage can feed a month of posts.

The heart of restaurant video is real food and real people. That part can't be faked, so film it for real.

Where the video should live

Not just Instagram. Your Google Business Profile takes videos up to 30 seconds long, and they show up only after your profile is verified (Google's photo and video rules). That puts your food on your Google profile, where people are picking a place to eat. Your website should carry the video too, on a page that says in plain text what you serve and where. My take: a great video on a page Google can't understand is a billboard in a basement.

Questions people actually ask

How long should a restaurant promo video be?

My take: for social feeds, about 15 to 30 seconds, because people decide fast. Google Business Profile videos can be up to 30 seconds. A website video can run about a minute. Save longer videos for a real story, like how the family started the place. Nobody watches a three-minute menu.

Can I just film my restaurant with a phone?

Yes, for everyday social posts. Real and current beats polished and old. My take: paid production earns its cost on one main video, well shot and well edited, that lives on your site and profile for a year and sets the tone for everything else.

Do restaurant videos actually bring in customers?

My take: video of real food is one of the strongest things a restaurant can post, because it answers two questions: does this look good, and does this place feel right? Hunger plus trust. Generic footage answers neither, which is why some videos do nothing.

What's the biggest restaurant video mistake?

My take: making it about the building instead of the food and the people. Close second: posting it once on one platform and letting it die. One shoot can become a main video plus a month of clips for your Google profile, your site, and social.
