Website Development

Site looks dated and the phone's quiet? What a website redesign costs, and when it's worth it.

My estimate: anywhere from a few hundred dollars for a cleanup to tens of thousands for a full agency rebuild. But the better first question is this: does your site need a new look, or does it need to be easier for customers and Google to understand?

The redesign price tiers, honestly

A refresh (new look, same structure). My estimate: a few hundred to a few thousand dollars from a freelancer. It's fast, but if the structure is the problem, you've painted a house with a cracked foundation.

A rebuild (new structure, new pages, reworked content). My estimate: a few thousand dollars from a solo builder to five figures from an agency. My take: this is where real improvement usually happens, because the structure is usually what's broken.

A migration (moving off a slow platform entirely). The price varies the most. My take: it's often worth it, because dropping monthly platform fees and plugin upkeep saves money every year after.

When a redesign is the wrong purchase

My take: the reason owners give most often for a redesign is “it looks outdated.” But customers who leave usually leave because the site is slow, confusing on a phone, or doesn't answer their question. And search engines mostly go by your words, your structure, and your markup, not your color scheme.

Before paying for a redesign, get the real diagnosis: is the site failing at looks, at speed, at answering questions, or at being readable to machines? Each has a different fix and a very different price.

The question to ask before paying anyone

“What will this redesign change about how customers find me?” If the answer is only about looks (colors, animations, a modern feel), you're buying decoration. A redesign worth paying for changes structure: pages that match what customers search, faster loading, machine-readable markup, and clear answers to real questions.

Questions people actually ask

How often should a website be redesigned?

There's no set schedule. Redesign when something you can measure is failing: slow loading, a broken phone layout, pages that don't match what customers search, or a structure search engines can't read. A site doing its job doesn't expire. My take: “It's been three years” is a designer's reason, not a business one.

Can I redesign my website without losing my Google ranking?

Nobody can promise your rankings, but careful work protects them: keep URLs where you can, redirect the ones that change, and keep the content that already ranks. Google recommends permanent redirects from old URLs to new ones and says to expect some temporary ups and downs during a move. Ask any builder how they handle redirects; a blank stare is your answer.

Is it cheaper to redesign or rebuild from scratch?

My take: rebuilding as a simple static site is often cheaper than redesigning a heavy platform site again and again, and it can cut monthly platform fees and plugin upkeep afterward. Get both quotes and compare the five-year cost, not just the first invoice.

What should a small business website redesign include?

My take: a structure that matches what customers search, fast pages, a layout built for phones first, schema markup so Google can read the business, and answers to the questions customers really ask. Visual polish comes last, because it's the easiest part to change later.
