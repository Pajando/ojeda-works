Website Development

How much does a website redesign cost?

Commonly anywhere from a few hundred dollars for a cleanup to tens of thousands for a full rebuild — and the honest answer starts with a different question: does your site need a redesign, or does it need to be readable?

The redesign price tiers, honestly

A refresh — new look on the same structure — commonly runs a few hundred to a few thousand dollars from a freelancer. Fast, but if the underlying structure is the problem, you've repainted a house with a cracked foundation.

A rebuild — new structure, new pages, content reworked — commonly runs a few thousand from a solo builder to five figures from an agency. This is where real improvement happens, because structure is usually what's actually broken.

A migration — moving off a slow platform entirely — varies most, and is often worth more than it costs: escaping monthly platform fees and plugin maintenance pays back every year afterward.

When a redesign is the wrong purchase

The most common reason owners want a redesign is “it looks outdated.” But customers rarely leave over aesthetics — they leave because the site is slow, confusing on a phone, or doesn't answer their question. And Google and AI models never see your design at all; they read your structure, your text, and your markup.

Before paying for a redesign, get the real diagnosis: is the site failing at looks, at speed, at answering questions, or at being readable to machines? Each has a different fix and a very different price.

The question to ask before paying anyone

“What will this redesign change about how customers find me?” If the answer is entirely about appearance — colors, animations, a modern feel — you're buying decoration. A redesign worth paying for changes structure: pages that match what customers search, faster loading, machine-readable markup, and clear answers to real questions.

Questions people actually ask

How often should a website be redesigned?

There's no schedule — redesign when something measurable is failing: slow loading, broken mobile layout, pages that don't match what customers search, or structure machines can't read. A site doing its job doesn't expire. “It's been three years” is a designer's reason, not a business one.

Can I redesign my website without losing my Google ranking?

Yes, if it's done carefully — keeping URLs where possible, redirecting the ones that change, and preserving the content that already ranks. Rankings commonly drop after careless redesigns because pages Google knew simply vanished. Ask any builder how they handle redirects; a blank stare is your answer.

Is it cheaper to redesign or rebuild from scratch?

Counterintuitively, rebuilding on a simple static foundation is often cheaper than repeatedly redesigning a bloated platform site — and it eliminates monthly platform fees and plugin maintenance afterward. Get both quotes and compare five-year cost, not just the invoice.

What should a small business website redesign include?

Structure matched to what customers actually search, fast static pages, mobile-first layout, schema markup so Google and AI models can read the business, and answers to the questions customers really ask. Visual polish comes last — it's the cheapest part to change later.
