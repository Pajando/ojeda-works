How much do Instagram ads cost?

You set the budget — a few dollars a day is technically enough. What you actually pay for results depends on your ad, your audience, and your offer. Here's the honest math, and when Instagram is the right pick.

How the pricing works

Instagram ads run through the same auction as Facebook (both are Meta): you bid for attention against everyone targeting the same people, paying per click or per thousand views. Costs swing by industry, season, and — most of all — whether people engage with your ad. Meta charges less to show ads people respond to, so the creative is your biggest cost lever, not the budget slider.

A realistic small-business start: a few hundred dollars of test budget over a few weeks, measured against one number — what a real customer costs you.

When Instagram beats Facebook

Instagram skews visual and younger; it rewards businesses that look good in motion — food, beauty, fitness, events, retail, anything with a before-and-after. Facebook's strength is broader local reach and community targeting. Since one Meta campaign can run on both, the honest play is letting placement data decide: run both placements, watch where customers (not clicks) come from, then concentrate.

The mistake that wastes Instagram budgets

Running polished-but-generic creative. Instagram users scroll past anything that smells like an ad from nowhere; real footage of your actual work, place, or people commonly outperforms studio gloss. And the foundation rule still applies: an ad pointing at a weak profile or slow site subsidizes a leak — fix the destination before paying for traffic.

Questions people actually ask

What's the minimum daily budget for Instagram ads?

Meta's technical minimums are commonly a dollar or a few per day per ad set. Practically, budget enough to learn — spread too thin, the algorithm never gathers enough data to optimize and you learn nothing. Fewer campaigns with real budget beat many starved ones.

Are Instagram ads worth it for a local business?

For visual local businesses — often yes, especially paired with location targeting and real footage. For urgent-need services (plumbing, repair), search ads usually win because intent matters more than inspiration. Match the platform to how your customers actually decide.

Why are my Instagram ads getting likes but no sales?

Engagement isn't intent. Commonly the offer is missing or vague, the link points somewhere generic, or the audience is set for reach instead of action. Optimize the campaign for conversions, tighten the offer, and judge on cost per customer — never on likes.

Do I need a business account to run Instagram ads?

Yes — a professional account connected to Meta's ad tools. It's free, and it also unlocks insights and the ability to respond as a business. Complete the profile fully first; the profile is where ad-curious people land before deciding.
