Paying for likes, not customers? What Instagram ads really cost.

You're paying for ads and the phone still isn't ringing. Here's the deal: you pick the budget. Meta's own pricing page says to start with at least $5 and run the ad for more than six days. What a real customer costs you depends on your ad, your audience and your offer. Here's how to think about it, and when Instagram is the right pick.

How the pricing works

Instagram and Facebook ads are both sold by Meta, and they share one pricing page. Meta says your cost depends on a few things: ad quality, your bidding strategy, your performance goal, a daily or lifetime budget, and your campaign objective. One surprise to know about: on a daily budget, Meta says it may spend up to 75% over on some days, but your week won't go over seven times your daily budget. My take: of everything on that list, the ad itself is the part you control most. A better ad does more for you than moving the budget slider.

My take: start with a test budget you'd be OK losing. Run it for a few weeks and watch one number: what a real customer costs you.

When Instagram beats Facebook

My take: Instagram is a good fit for businesses that look good in motion. Think food, beauty, fitness, events, retail, anything with a before-and-after. You don't have to guess, though. Meta says you can use Ads Manager for “multi-platform campaigns” (Meta for Business). So run the ad on both apps, see where your customers come from (customers, not clicks), and put your money there.

The mistake that wastes Instagram budgets

A shiny ad that could be for any business. My take: people scroll right past anything that looks like an ad from nowhere. Real footage of your actual work, your place or your people looks like you, and that's what gets someone to stop. Also fix where the ad sends people before you pay for clicks. If your profile is half empty or your site is slow, you're paying to send people into a leak.

Quick answers

What's the minimum daily budget for Instagram ads?

Meta's pricing page says to start with at least $5 and pick a run longer than six days, so its system can find the best people for your ad. My take: don't split a small budget across lots of ads. A few ads with enough money behind them teach you more than many starved ones.

Are Instagram ads worth it for a local business?

My take: often yes for businesses people like to look at, like food, beauty or events, especially with location targeting and real footage. For urgent jobs like a burst pipe or a broken car, I'd start with search ads. That person is looking for help right now, not scrolling for ideas. Match the ad to how your customers actually decide.

Why are my Instagram ads getting likes but no sales?

Likes aren't buyers. My take: look for three things. The offer is missing or vague, the link goes somewhere generic, or the campaign is set to get seen instead of to get action. Meta says your objective “plays a large role” in what your ads cost (Meta pricing page), so pick the one that matches what you want: calls, messages or sales. Then sharpen the offer and judge the ad by what a customer costs you, never by likes.

Do I need a business account to run Instagram ads?

Meta changes its setup rules, so go by what Ads Manager asks for when you build the ad. My take: switch to a professional account before you spend anything, and fill out the profile completely. When someone taps your ad, your profile is what they check before they decide.
