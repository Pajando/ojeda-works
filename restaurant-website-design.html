Website Development

Hungry people picking the place next door? Restaurant website design that gets them to your table.

A restaurant website has one job: turn a hungry person into a seated one. Menu, hours, location, photos, and a way to act, fast, on a phone. Everything else is extra. Here's the honest guide.

The five things that matter

The menu as real text, not a PDF (PDFs are slow to zoom on a phone). Hours that match your Google profile exactly. One tap to get directions or call. Photos of real food from your real kitchen. And one clear button: reserve, order, or call. My take: a site that gets these five right on a phone beats any fancy scrolling effects or video backgrounds.

What it commonly costs

My take on cost: website builders charge a monthly fee, so check their pricing pages before you sign up. A freelancer can build a proper restaurant site for a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, and agencies usually charge more for much the same five jobs. Watch out for platforms that lock your menu into their system and charge every month forever. A simple, fast site with a text menu costs little to run, and real text is what Google and AI apps can read.

Why this feeds your Google and AI presence

When someone asks Google or an AI app for “best tacos near me,” the answer is built from what those systems can read: your site's text, your hours, your reviews, your Google profile. Google can index PDFs (Google's list of file types it can index), but my take is that a text menu on the page is the safer bet, and a photo-only site gives machines almost nothing to read. Pair the readable site with a video of the food: a restaurant promo video and a clear site help each other.

Questions people actually ask

Should my restaurant menu be a PDF?

No. PDFs are slow on phones and painful to pinch and zoom. Google can read PDFs, but my take is that real text on the page, with prices, is easier for people and for AI apps. Keep a print-ready PDF for the printer, not the website.

Do I need online ordering on my website?

My take: only if you do a lot of takeout. Then yes, and pick a system where you keep the customer list instead of a marketplace that owns it. If you're mostly dine-in, a reservation link and a call button do the job without online-ordering fees.

How many pages does a restaurant website need?

My take: three to five. Home (with menu link, hours, and address at the top), menu, and contact/location. Add about or catering/events only if they're real. One great page beats ten thin ones; hungry people don't browse, they decide.

Why doesn't my restaurant show up when people search?

Common reasons: the Google Business Profile is incomplete, the hours don't match the site, the menu is only a PDF or a photo, or the site never says plainly what you serve and where. All fixable without ads. Fixing them makes you easier for Google and AI apps to read, but nobody can promise you a ranking.
