Missed the call, lost the job? Plumber marketing that gets you the call, honestly.

Picture it: water on the kitchen floor at 9pm. They search, they call the first plumber who looks real, and if nobody picks up, they call the next one. Here's how to be the plumber who gets that job.

Win the flooded-kitchen search

Someone searching “plumber near me” at 9pm needs to see a few things fast. First, a complete Google Business Profile with your real hours and service area. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search (Google Business Profile Help). Keep it to one profile with your service area set, not one per town. Google allows one profile per business (Google Business Profile guidelines). Then recent reviews, and a phone that gets answered. I think of missed calls as the quiet leak in a plumbing business, so if you can't pick up on a job, set up an answering service or a fast call-back habit. If you offer 24/7, say it everywhere. If you don't, honest hours beat a broken promise.

Get reviews on every job, not once a year

Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking (Google Business Profile Help). My take: someone in a panic reads the newest reviews first, so a steady few every month beats a big pile from years ago. Build the ask into the end of the job: it's fixed, it's tested, the customer is relieved. Send a one-tap link and let them say it in their own words. A review that says “drain cleared in Napa this week” costs you nothing.

Projects, maintenance, and the lead math

Not every job is an emergency. My take: water heaters, repipes and remodels are jobs people research before they call. Give each one its own page on your site that explains the options in plain words and gives honest price ranges. Maintenance plans, like a yearly water-heater flush or an inspection, give a one-time customer a reason to call you first next time. Be careful with lead sites. A shared lead can go to more than one plumber, so you can end up in a price fight. Track what each booked job costs you from every source before you renew anything.

Questions people actually ask

How do plumbers get more emergency calls?

Be easy to find, easy to trust and easy to reach when it matters. That means a complete Google profile with your real hours, recent reviews, and a phone that gets answered. My take: picking up is marketing too. A call you miss at 9pm is often a job someone else gets.

Are Google ads worth it for plumbers?

They can be. Emergency searches come from people who need help now, and those clicks can cost real money. Start small, keep the ads inside your service area, and judge them on booked jobs, not clicks. I'd run them on top of a complete profile and steady reviews, not instead of them.

How do plumbers get reviews fast?

Ask right when the problem is fixed and tested, while the customer is relieved. Send a one-tap link and let them use their own words. Make it a habit on every job, not a once-a-year push. Never buy or fake reviews. Trust is what you're selling.

Should a plumber offer maintenance plans?

I'd say yes, if you can keep up with them. Inspections, flushes and priority-response plans can bring steadier money between emergencies. The bigger win is the relationship. A plan customer already has your number when the next pipe bursts.
