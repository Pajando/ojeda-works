People scroll past your video? How long should a promo video be, honestly.

You paid for a video and people scroll right past it. My take: 15 to 30 seconds for feeds, 30 to 90 seconds for your website and Google profile, and longer only when you have a real story to tell. Length is often not the real problem, though. Here's the honest breakdown.

The honest numbers by placement

Social feeds (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook): my pick is 15 to 30 seconds, with the point up front. Thumbs move fast, and nobody owes you a second look.

Your website and Google Business Profile: my pick is 30 to 90 seconds. Someone already deciding about you will give you a minute, as long as every part keeps proving something.

Ads: keep them short and get to the point fast. Meta says it generally recommends a 15 second maximum for in-stream video ads (Meta video ads). On YouTube, people can skip a skippable ad after 5 seconds (Google Ads Help), so your point has to land before that.

The problem the question usually hides

My take: when a video feels too long, the runtime is rarely the problem. The video just stops proving anything partway through. Sixty seconds that keep answering “why this business?” hold attention. Twenty seconds of logo animation don't. Cut everything that isn't proof, and the right length shows up on its own.

One shoot, every length

Plan one shoot that gives you every cut: a 15-second feed version, a 60-second version for your site and profile, and a handful of short clips for the month. Then length stops being a big decision. It's just which version you export.

Quick answers

How long should a promo video be on Instagram?

My take: 15 to 30 seconds for a promo, with the hook right at the start. Longer Reels can work when the content really holds people, like a process video or a before-and-after. A sales message past 30 seconds is fighting the scroll.

Is a 2-minute promo video too long?

For an ad, usually yes. Meta generally recommends 15 seconds max for in-stream video ads. On your own website, two minutes can work for a real founding story or a detailed walkthrough. A message you could say in 45 seconds can't hold two minutes. My take: length isn't the sin. Padding is.

How long before viewers drop off?

It depends on the platform and the video, and I won't give you one made-up number. Here's what the platforms say. Google's video ad guidance tells you to “get to the heart of the story faster” (Google Ads Help), and YouTube viewers can skip a skippable ad after 5 seconds. My take: if your video opens with a logo and music before saying anything, you've spent your best seconds on nothing.

Should I make different lengths for different platforms?

Yes, and plan it before you shoot, not after. One shoot can give you a short feed cut, a 60-second version and clips. My take: shooting for one length and trimming later is how videos end up feeling chopped instead of built.
