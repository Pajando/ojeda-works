Customers forget you between visits? Email marketing for small business that brings them back.

Past customers forget you and don't come back? Email reaches the people who already said yes to hearing from you, with no social feed deciding who sees it. It can start free, and here's a setup small enough to keep.

Why email is worth it

Your social posts reach whoever an algorithm allows. Your email is sent straight to the people who gave you their address: customers who already trust you. My take: that's where a lot of repeat business comes from. My take: your customer list is one of the most overlooked things a small business owns, because it's full of people who might come back with a nudge.

What it costs

You can start free. Mailchimp's free plan allows up to 250 contacts and 500 emails a month (Mailchimp pricing, checked October 2026), and paid plans cost more as your list grows. The real cost is your time. My take: build a system small enough to keep going, like one email a month, written like a note from the owner, with one useful thing in it. That beats a big weekly newsletter you quit after a few weeks.

How to build the list without being annoying

Ask at a natural moment, like checkout, booking or the invoice, with a plain reason: “Want a heads-up when we run specials?” Never buy lists. My take: people on a bought list don't know you, so they're more likely to mark you as spam, and that can hurt delivery to the customers who do want your emails. Every marketing email also needs a clear way to opt out, even when it goes to another business. The FTC says each email that breaks the CAN-SPAM Act can bring penalties of up to $53,088.

Questions people actually ask

How often should a small business send emails?

My take: once a month is a good start. It's enough to stay in mind and easy enough to keep up in busy seasons. Frequency matters less than reliability and usefulness: a short note with one real thing (an offer, a tip, a heads-up) beats an elaborate newsletter that ships twice and dies.

What should a small business email actually say?

What a good regular would want to know: a seasonal special, a new service, a useful tip in your specialty, a reminder tied to the calendar. Written like a person, signed by a person. My take: skip the corporate template look. A plain note from the owner feels more real.

Is email marketing free?

It can start free. Mailchimp lists a free plan for up to 250 contacts and 500 emails a month, checked October 2026. You'll pay as the list grows, which is the good problem. The real investment is the habit, which is why the system must be small enough to keep.

Can I just email customers from my regular inbox?

For one-to-one, yes. For bulk marketing email, use an email platform. The FTC says every commercial email must tell people how to opt out, and opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days; a platform makes that easier. My take: blasting a list from your personal inbox can push your everyday email into spam folders.
