Email marketing for a small business, honestly.

Email is the channel you own — no algorithm decides who sees it. For a small business it commonly costs little to nothing, and it outperforms social for actually driving repeat business. Here's the honest setup.

Why email still wins

Your social posts reach whoever an algorithm allows. Your email reaches everyone who gave you their address — customers who already trust you, which is exactly who repeat business comes from. For most small businesses the list is the most undervalued asset they have: every past customer who'd return with a nudge.

What it costs

Most platforms (Mailchimp, Brevo, and similar) are free or nearly free at small-list sizes, commonly climbing to $20–$100+ monthly as the list grows. The real cost is time — which is why the honest advice is a system small enough to survive: one email a month, written like a note from the owner, with one useful thing in it. That beats an ambitious weekly newsletter that dies in six weeks.

How to build the list without being annoying

Ask at the natural moment — checkout, booking, invoice — with a plain reason: “want a heads-up when we run specials?” Never buy lists: bought addresses don't know you, flag you as spam, and poison your deliverability for the people who do. Slow and real compounds; fast and fake backfires.

Questions people actually ask

How often should a small business send emails?

Commonly once a month — enough to stay in mind, sustainable enough to survive busy seasons. Frequency matters less than reliability and usefulness: a short note with one real thing (an offer, a tip, a heads-up) beats an elaborate newsletter that ships twice and dies.

What should a small business email actually say?

What a good regular would want to know: a seasonal special, a new service, a useful tip in your specialty, a reminder tied to the calendar. Written like a person, signed by a person. Skip the corporate template look — plain emails from the owner routinely outperform designed ones.

Is email marketing free?

Nearly, at small scale — major platforms have free tiers covering hundreds of subscribers. You'll pay as the list grows, which is the good problem. The real investment is the habit, which is why the system must be small enough to keep.

Can I just email customers from my regular inbox?

For one-to-one, yes. For any bulk sending, use a real platform — it handles unsubscribe links and spam-law compliance (required), and it keeps your business address's reputation safe. Bulk mail from a personal inbox risks landing your everyday email in spam folders.
