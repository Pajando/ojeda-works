Sick of posting every day while the phone stays quiet? Marketing without social media: what actually works.

You post, nobody comments, and the phone stays quiet. If keeping up a daily feed is why your marketing keeps stalling, here's some relief. My take: for a lot of local service businesses, social media isn't where most new customers come from. Search, reviews, email and referrals can carry the load. Here's how, plus the one trade-off to be honest about.

The channels that outwork posting

My take: search works differently from social. Instead of interrupting people who weren't looking, it shows up when they need you. Start with a complete Google Business Profile, reviews you ask for every time, and a website that answers real questions in plain text. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local results, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking (Google Business Profile Help). Add email: a simple list of past customers, written to like a person a few times a year (“gutter season is coming, want on the schedule?”). My take: it can bring back repeat work, and the list is yours, not a platform's. Last, referrals. Ask at the happy moment and say thank you. No gimmicks needed.

What machines read when you don't post

My take: when someone searches for a local business, your website, your Google profile, your reviews and what other sites say about you matter more than your feed. Google says there's no special trick needed to show up in its AI features, and that it's worth making sure your important content is in text form (Google Search Central). So if you skip social, put that time into a website and profile that answer questions while you work.

The one honest trade-off

What you give up without social: people half-knowing you before they ever search, and a steady stream of proof for trades where looks matter (food, hair, tattoos, flowers, detailing). For those visual businesses, skipping social has a real cost. My take: the middle ground is a portfolio habit, not a content habit. Post finished work when you have it, no pressure otherwise, and let your profile carry the rest. For everyone else, like plumbers, accountants, movers and consultants, my take is that strong reviews and a website that answers questions will do more for you than a feed you can't keep up.

Questions people actually ask

Can a business really succeed without social media?

My take: locally, yes. For many service businesses, search, reviews, email and referrals can bring in steady work. The businesses that really need a feed are the visual ones, and even there, posting finished work beats a posting schedule.

What should I do instead of posting on social media?

Complete your Google profile, ask every happy customer for a review, build a website that answers the questions customers search, and email past customers a few times a year. My take: that's a better use of the hours you were spending on posts.

Do search engines or AI assistants care about my social media?

My take: less than your website, your Google profile, your reviews and what other sites say about you. Google says complete, accurate info makes you more likely to show up in local results, and more reviews can help local ranking. I haven't seen Google say that posting on social does.

I hate posting but my competitors are everywhere. Am I losing?

Check where it counts. Search for your service in your town, see who shows up, and compare reviews. My take: a competitor with lots of reels but few reviews and a thin website is weak at the moment someone picks who to call. You can win that moment without posting.
