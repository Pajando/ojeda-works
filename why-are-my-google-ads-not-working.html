Why are my Google Ads not working?

Nine times out of ten it's one of six boring causes — and Google publishes the list. The tenth time it's the cause nobody checks: the ads work fine, and what's broken is the page the click lands on. Here's the checklist in the order that finds the problem fastest.

Run Google's own list first

Google's own troubleshooting guide covers the mechanical causes: the campaign or ad is paused or removed, billing failed, the daily budget is depleted by lunchtime, the ad is still under review or got disapproved, bids are below what the auction takes, or the targeting is drawn so tight almost nobody qualifies. Check status columns before theories — “paused since March” has ended more mysteries than any strategy session. Two more Google-documented gotchas: new campaigns and freshly-edited ones sit in a learning phase for days, so constant fiddling resets the clock; and searching for your own ad repeatedly without clicking teaches Google to stop showing it to you — use the Ad Preview tool instead.

Then check the math the dashboard hides

Ads that run but produce nothing usually fail on economics, not settings. Broad keywords burn budget on searches that were never buyers (“free,” “jobs,” “DIY” variants) — the search-terms report shows exactly what you paid for; read it and add the negatives. Bidding on clicks you can't afford to win halfway is worse than not bidding: $200 of budget in a $30-per-click market buys six visitors and no data. Tight and boring beats broad and hopeful — your services, your towns, and the searches where someone is ready to hire.

The cause nobody checks: where the click lands

If ads deliver clicks and the phone stays quiet, the ads aren't the problem — the landing experience is. A slow page, a homepage instead of a service page, no visible phone number, no proof (reviews, photos, prices) — the click bounces, and no bid strategy fixes that. Before raising budget, fix what the click lands on; before running ads at all, make sure the free foundation — profile, reviews, a site that answers real questions — converts. Paid traffic pointed at weakness just spends faster. If that foundation check sounds like the harder problem, that's the one we actually solve.

Questions people actually ask

Why are my Google Ads getting impressions but no clicks?

The ad shows for searches it shouldn't, or says nothing worth clicking. Read the search-terms report, add negative keywords, and rewrite the ad to name the service, the place, and a reason to pick you. Generic ads get generic ignoring.

Why are my Google Ads getting clicks but no calls?

The landing page is losing them: slow load, wrong page, no phone number visible, no proof. Send clicks to a service page with reviews, prices, and a tap-to-call number — the ad's job ends at the click; the page does the converting.

How long should I run Google Ads before judging them?

New and heavily-edited campaigns spend days in Google's learning phase, so judge after a few weeks of stable settings — but judge on booked work per dollar, not impressions or clicks. No tracked conversions after meaningful spend is a verdict too.

Should I just hire someone to run my Google Ads?

If nobody's reading the search-terms report monthly, you're paying for waste either way. Whoever runs them — you, us, anyone — hold the ads to one number: what a booked customer costs. An account manager who can't answer that is decoration.
