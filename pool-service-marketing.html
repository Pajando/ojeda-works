Pool service marketing: the honest playbook.

Pool service is a route business: the prize isn't a job, it's a weekly account worth thousands a year that compounds quietly. Every marketing decision should be judged by one metric — does it add reliable recurring accounts on streets you already drive?

Sell reliability, because that's what they're buying

Homeowners fire pool guys for one reason: they stopped showing up. So make reliability visible before the sale — service-day photo reports (“chemicals balanced, filter cleaned, gate latched”), reviews that say “same day every week for three years,” a real business with a real truck. The photo-report habit is the single best marketing tool in this industry: it retains the account, and homeowners forward those reports to neighbors asking for a pool guy.

Density is profit — market by street

Ten accounts in one neighborhood beat twenty scattered across the county; windshield time eats route businesses alive. So aim the marketing tight: door hangers on the streets you already service (“we're at three homes on this block every Tuesday”), neighbor referral incentives, Nextdoor presence in your actual zones. When a route fills a neighborhood, raise the line for new areas or sell the outliers.

The foundation and what to skip

Google Business Profile per service area with photos of real work — green-to-clean transformations are the industry's before-and-after gold. A simple site with services, service-area map, and honest monthly pricing ranges in readable text; “pool service near me” and “how much is weekly pool service” get asked to AI assistants all summer. What to skip: pricing low to win accounts you'll resent (route math punishes thin margins forever), and buying shared leads at the county level when your profit lives on five streets.

Questions people actually ask

How do pool service companies get customers?

Neighbor referrals and search, concentrated deliberately: visible weekly reliability, service reports owners can forward, reviews mentioning years of consistency, and marketing aimed at streets already on the route.

How much should a pool company charge for weekly service?

Price for route math: drive time, chemicals, and a margin that survives repairs and summer churn. Winning accounts on lowball monthly rates locks in resentment — full-service pricing with visible reports retains better than cheap.

Do green-to-clean photos really work as marketing?

Yes — they're the most convincing proof in the industry. A swamp becoming a swimming pool in four days demonstrates competence no slogan can. Post them everywhere, with the timeline honest.

Should a pool service use door hangers?

In this business, yes — targeted at streets you already service, mentioning the neighbors' service day. It's route-density marketing: the cheapest account to serve is the one next door to an existing one.
