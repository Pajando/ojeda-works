Losing accounts or driving all over the county? Pool service marketing for a tighter route, honestly.

A customer quits, and that's a weekly account gone, not one job. Pool service is a route business, so the prize is a steady account on a street you already drive. My take: judge every marketing idea by one question. Does it add accounts that stay, close to the stops you already have?

Sell reliability, because that's what they're buying

My take: the fastest way to lose a pool account is to miss a week and say nothing. So make showing up easy to see. Send a quick photo report after every visit (“chemicals balanced, filter cleaned, gate latched”). When you ask for a review, let customers use their own words. I think one that mentions years of showing up on the same day every week is the kind a new customer wants to read. A photo report also gives a happy customer something easy to forward when a neighbor asks who does their pool.

Market by the street, not the county

Every mile between stops is drive time nobody pays you for. That's why a tight route can be worth more than a big, scattered one. Aim your marketing close to home: door hangers on the streets you already service (“we're on this street every Tuesday”), a thank-you for customers who refer a neighbor, and Nextdoor posts in the areas you actually cover. Never point out a customer's home without their OK. Once a neighborhood fills up, you can charge more for far-away areas or let the far-out accounts go.

The foundation and what to skip

Set up one Google Business Profile with your service area marked. If you travel to customers from one base, Google allows one service-area profile, not one per town (Google Business Profile guidelines). Fill it out completely and add photos of real work. I think green-to-clean before-and-afters are the best proof you can post. Then build a simple website with your services, the towns you cover, and honest monthly price ranges written out as text. Google says its AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special tricks, and its list of basic practices includes making important content available as text (Google Search Central). What to skip: pricing low to win accounts you'll end up resenting, and buying shared leads for the whole county when your profit lives on a few streets.

Questions people actually ask

How do pool service companies get customers?

The two I'd focus on are neighbor referrals and search. Show up on the same day every week, send service reports customers can forward, ask for reviews, and aim your marketing at streets already on your route.

How much should a pool company charge for weekly service?

I can't give you one number that fits every town. Build your price from your own route: drive time, chemicals, and a margin that still works when repairs and off-season cancellations hit. I wouldn't win accounts with a lowball rate. A fair price with a photo report after every visit is easier to defend.

Do green-to-clean photos really work as marketing?

I'd say yes. A green pool turning clear shows what you can do better than any slogan. Post them on your Google profile, your site and social, and be honest about how long it took.

Should a pool service use door hangers?

Yes, if you aim them at streets you already service. Mention the day you're already on that street, without naming any customer's home. A new account next door to one you have adds almost no drive time.
