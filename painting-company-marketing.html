Content & Social

Losing bids to the other painter? Painting company marketing that wins the estimate, honestly.

You walked the house, sent the bid, and never heard back. My take: when a homeowner compares painters, they're asking who will show up, protect their floors, and not vanish with the deposit. So your marketing has one job: look like the safe pick before you walk in for the estimate.

Proof of work beats promises

Every job is proof if you take the photos: the prep and masking, the before and after in the same light, the clean line at the ceiling. Add new ones to your Google Business Profile every week or two so it shows recent work. Label each photo with the job and the town, never the street address, and ask the homeowner before you post their house. My take: a stack of real job photos says more than any slogan about quality.

The estimate is the moment of truth

My take: a lot of painting jobs are lost before the price even arrives. A slow reply, a vague walkthrough, a one-line number. Answer inquiries the same day. Show up when you said you would. Send a written estimate that spells out the prep, the number of coats and the paint you'll use. The homeowner figures the painter who explains things during the bid will keep explaining during the job. That can win the job even when you're not the cheapest.

The foundation and what to skip

Fill out your profile completely: services, service area and hours. Put your license number and proof of insurance on your site and on every estimate. Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results (Google Business Profile Help). Ask for reviews that mention the crew and the clean-up. Then build a simple site with photos, your services (interior, exterior, cabinets) and the towns you work, written as plain text. Google says its AI search features need no special optimization, and it lists putting important content in text form among the basics (Google Search Central). What to skip: lead sites as your only source, since a shared lead means you're bidding against other painters for the same job, and panic discounts in winter. My take: market cabinet and interior work in the fall to fill those months instead.

Questions people actually ask

How do painting companies get customers?

My take: referrals, repeat clients and search. Real job photos and reviews that name the crew help you look like the safe pick. Same-day replies and detailed written estimates help you win the bid. Lead sites can fill gaps, but you'll often be competing on price.

Should a painter pay for leads from HomeAdvisor or Angi?

To fill gaps, maybe. As your whole plan, no. A shared lead goes to more than one company, so you're bidding against others for the same job, and the fees add up. Build your profile and your referrals so bought leads become optional.

What should a painting company post on social media?

Before and afters, close-ups of the prep work, color choices, and your crew at work. Tag the town. My take: people often think about a paint job for a while before they call, so be the painter whose work they've already seen.

How much should a painting company spend on marketing?

There's no single right number, so I won't make one up. Early on, spend time before money: photos, reviews and fast replies cost nothing. My take: ads work better pointed at a profile full of proof than at an empty one. Track what each job costs you and spend more where the jobs come from.
