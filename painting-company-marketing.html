Painting company marketing: the honest playbook.

Homeowners collect three painting bids and pick the one they trust to show up, mask properly, and not disappear with the deposit. Your marketing has one job: be the company that already looks trustworthy before the estimate visit.

Proof of work beats promises

Every job is a portfolio piece if you shoot it: the prep and masking (that's what pros notice), the before-and-after in the same light, the clean lines at the ceiling. Post them to your Google Business Profile constantly — that's what a homeowner sees when comparing three companies at 9pm. A hundred real job photos with addresses' neighborhoods named beats any slogan about quality.

The estimate is the moment of truth

Most painters lose jobs before the bid arrives — they respond in three days, show up vague, and send a one-line price. Answer inquiries the same day. Show up when you said. Send a written estimate that itemizes prep, coats, and product, because the competing bids won't. The company that communicates during the sale is assumed to communicate during the job, and that assumption wins bids at a higher price.

The foundation and what to skip

Profile complete with license number, insurance, service area, and reviews that mention crew names and clean-up. A simple site with photos, services (interior, exterior, cabinets), and the towns you work — in text search engines and AI assistants can read, because “best painter in [town]” is asked to ChatGPT now. What to skip: lead-selling platforms as your only source — you're bidding against five companies for a stranger who picked the lowest number — and winter panic-discounting; fill the calendar instead with cabinet and interior work marketed in fall.

Questions people actually ask

How do painting companies get customers?

Referrals, repeat clients, and search — in that order. Photos of real jobs and reviews naming the crew win the comparison; same-day responses and detailed written estimates win the bid. Lead platforms can fill gaps but punish you on price.

Should a painter pay for leads from HomeAdvisor or Angi?

As a supplement, maybe — as the whole plan, no. Shared leads mean bidding against several companies for price shoppers, and the fees compound. Build the profile and referral engine so bought leads become optional.

What should a painting company post on social media?

Job transformations, prep work close-ups, color consultations, the crew. Tag the town. Homeowners planning a repaint scroll for months — be the company whose work they've already seen fifty times.

How much should a painting company spend on marketing?

Many run on five percent of revenue or less once referrals compound. Early on, spend time before money: photos, reviews, and response speed are free and outperform ads pointed at an empty profile.
