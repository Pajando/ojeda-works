How much does a logo cost?

Anywhere from five dollars to fifty thousand — the widest price range in all of design. Here's what each tier actually buys, and the honest answer for what a small business needs.

The tiers, honestly

Marketplaces and AI generators ($5–$100): you get a mark, fast. The risks are genericness and recycled elements — your logo may quietly look like a hundred others, and licensing of stock elements can be murky.

Freelance designers (a few hundred to a few thousand): the sweet spot for most small businesses. You're paying for someone to actually think about your business before drawing, plus proper files and usage rights.

Agencies and brand studios (five figures and up): you're buying research, strategy, and a full identity system. Justified for companies whose brand carries serious equity; overkill for a business that needs to look credible and get found.

What actually drives the price

Revisions, research, and rights. A cheap logo skips all three: no discovery about your customers, no refinement rounds, and sometimes no clear ownership transfer. Ask any designer two questions — how many revision rounds are included, and do I own the final files outright, including the vector source? The answers separate professionals from mills.

What a small business actually needs

One mark that works small (a phone screen, a map pin) and big (a sign), in color and single-color, delivered as vector files you own. That's it. Elaborate brand books matter later; being recognizable and consistent everywhere your customers see you matters now. Spending a few hundred dollars once beats re-doing a five-dollar logo three times.

Questions people actually ask

Is a cheap AI-generated logo good enough to start?

For testing a business idea — genuinely yes. The tradeoffs are genericness, murky element licensing, and rework later once the business is real. If you go cheap, treat it as a placeholder decision, not a brand decision.

What files should I get with a logo?

Vector source files (SVG, AI, or EPS), plus PNG exports in color, white, and single-color versions. If a designer only hands you a JPG, you didn't buy a logo — you bought a picture of one, and every future use will fight you.

How many logo revisions are normal?

Commonly two to three structured rounds. Unlimited-revision offers usually mean the price already assumes churn, or the designer plans to wear you down to acceptance. Fewer, more thoughtful rounds with real reasoning beat endless tweaks.

Does my logo affect how I show up on Google?

Indirectly but really: your logo appears in search results, map listings, and AI answers through your business profile and site markup. Consistency — same mark everywhere, properly declared in your site's schema — is what search engines and AI reward. Prettiness matters less than consistency.
