Content & Social

Empty chairs between regulars? Barbershop marketing that fills them honestly.

Every cut you give walks around town with your work on it. That's free advertising, but only if people can find out who did it. Here's how to make that count, plus rebooking and a complete profile.

Claim the visual advantage

Every cut is content: the fade close-up, the before-and-after, the transformation clip, the lineup. Shoot at the chair with permission, post with the shop name and town, and load your best work to your Google Business Profile by the dozen. That's what someone sees when they search 'barbershop near me' and compare. My take: a profile full of real fades looks a lot better than a logo and two storefront photos.

Rebooking is the real engine

Hair grows on a schedule, so the repeat business is built in. My take: the best habit you can build is booking the next cut before the client leaves the chair. Add a reminder text when they don't, and a simple referral nudge (“bring your boy in”). Every client who rebooks is one less stranger you have to win that week.

The foundation and what to skip

Complete profile: hours (accurate ones), prices, booking link, reviews asked at the mirror moment in the client's own words. A one-page site with the same, readable by machines, so search engines and AI assistants can read it. What to skip: paying for generic ads before the profile is full, and discounting cuts. My take: barbershop loyalty runs on consistency and respect, not coupons.

Questions people actually ask

How do barbershops get new clients?

Visibly good work where searchers look: a profile stacked with real cuts and reviews, so you look good when people compare shops nearby. Then referrals, because happy regulars know other heads. My take: ads come last, and only once the profile is full.

Should a barbershop use a booking app?

If no-shows or phone interruptions cost you real time, yes. A booking link also gives people one more way to book from your profile and site. Keep walk-in space if that's your culture; the app serves the schedule, not the other way around.

What should a barbershop post on social media?

The work: fades, transformations, the chair-side process, the shop's energy. Tag the town. Client permission always. My take: two to four real posts a week beat daily filler, and one clean transformation clip says more than a month of memes.

Do barbershops need a website?

A simple one, yes: prices, hours, booking, photos, in text machines can read. A site is a page you own, and an app can change its rules on you. My take: five sections on one fast page covers most shops.
