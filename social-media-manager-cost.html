Posting and still no customers? How much does a social media manager cost, really?

My take: anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand dollars a month, depending on who does it and how much they really do. Here's how the pricing works, and the honest case for not paying anyone yet.

The three ways this gets priced

Freelancers: my take, not a survey, is a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars a month for a small business, depending on how many platforms and posts. Hourly rates vary a lot, so ask for a flat monthly price with a post count. That makes quotes easy to compare.

Agencies: my take is they usually cost more than a freelancer. Part of that pays for real strategy; part pays for the account manager between you and whoever writes the posts. Ask for the monthly price and exactly what's in it, in writing.

In-house means a salary. My take: that fits a business with nonstop content needs, and it's too much for most small shops.

What you're actually paying for

My take: posting is the part you see, and it's the least valuable part. What matters is whether the person knows your business well enough to say something true in your voice, and whether the posts answer questions your real customers ask instead of filling a calendar with generic quotes and holiday graphics.

Ask any candidate one question: “what would your first five posts for my business say?” If the answer could apply to any business in your category, keep looking.

When you shouldn't pay for this yet

Honest answer: if your Google Business Profile is missing info, your website doesn't answer basic customer questions, or you have only a handful of reviews, fix those first. The Google profile is free, and the rest is mostly your time. Google says “Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results” and “More reviews and positive ratings can help your business’s local ranking” (Google Business Profile Help). Social posts make a strong base louder; they don't replace one.

Questions people actually ask

Is a social media manager worth it for a small business?

Only after the basics are set: a complete Google Business Profile, a website people can read, and real reviews. Google says complete info makes a business more likely to show up in local search, and more reviews can help local ranking (Google Business Profile Help). My take: once those are in place, a good content person makes everything louder; before that, you're paying to decorate an empty store.

How many hours a week does social media management take?

My take: for a small business doing it right (a few posts a week, replies, and monthly planning), expect roughly 3 to 8 hours a week. Much more than that usually means the price includes ads or video production, which are different services with different prices. Ask what's included.

Can I manage social media myself?

Yes, and many owners should, at least at first. Nobody knows the business better. The real costs are showing up every week and knowing what to say. My take: a middle path that often works is paying once for a content plan and templates, then doing the posting yourself.

What should a social media manager actually deliver?

Posts in your real voice that answer real customer questions, replies within a business day, and a short monthly note on what worked. Be careful with reports full of impressions and reach. My take: the number that matters is whether customers say they saw you.
